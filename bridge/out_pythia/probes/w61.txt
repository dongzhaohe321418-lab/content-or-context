Ingrid Halvorsen-Maaby

Ingrid Halvorsen-Maaby (born 14 March 1991) is a Norwegian former cross-country skier and biathlete who competed internationally from 2010 to 2026. She won four Olympic medals across three Winter Games, including a gold medal in the women's 30 kilometre classic at the 2018 Winter Olympics in Pyeongchang. She also won eleven medals at the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships and claimed the overall Tour de Ski title in the 2019–20 season. Known for her strength in classic-technique distance racing and her unusually late switch to biathlon in the final years of her career, she is regarded as one of the more versatile Nordic athletes of her generation.

= = Early life = =

Halvorsen-Maaby was born in Tynset, a municipality in the Østerdalen valley of Innlandet county, Norway. Her father, Arne Halvorsen, was a forestry technician and amateur orienteer, and her mother, Kari Maaby, worked as a primary school teacher in nearby Alvdal. She was the second of three children. The family lived on a small farm several kilometres outside the town centre, and she has described learning to ski on the forest tracks behind the family property before she was three years old.

She joined the local sports club, Tynset IF, at the age of six, initially competing in both cross-country skiing and orienteering. In interviews she has credited orienteering with developing her pacing and route-reading skills, which she later applied to tactical decision-making in mass-start races. As a teenager she attended Nord-Østerdal videregående skole and later enrolled at the Norwegian College of Elite Sport (Norges Toppidrettsgymnas) in Lillehammer, where she trained under coach Torleif Bækken from 2007 to 2010.

At the Norwegian junior championships in 2009 she won the 5 kilometre classic and placed second in the pursuit, results that earned her selection to the national junior team.

= = Career = =

= = = Junior career and early senior years (2010–2013) = = =

Halvorsen-Maaby made her international debut at the 2010 FIS Nordic Junior World Ski Championships in Hinterzarten, Germany, where she finished fourth in the 5 kilometre classic and won a silver medal as part of the Norwegian 4 × 3.3 kilometre relay team. The following year, at the 2011 Junior World Championships in Otepää, Estonia, she won the 5 kilometre classic by eleven seconds, becoming the first skier from Hedmark county to win an individual junior world title.

She made her FIS Cross-Country World Cup debut in November 2011 at Sjusjøen, finishing 34th in the 10 kilometre freestyle. She scored her first World Cup points the following month in Davos, Switzerland, placing 22nd in a 10 kilometre classic event. Throughout the 2011–12 and 2012–13 seasons she alternated between the World Cup and the second-tier Scandinavian Cup, recording her first World Cup top-ten finish in January 2013 with a seventh place in the 10 kilometre classic in Liberec, Czech Republic.

Her development during this period was interrupted by a stress fracture in her left tibia, diagnosed in August 2012, which kept her out of on-snow training for approximately ten weeks. She later stated that the injury led her to reorganise her training around a greater share of roller skiing and cycling at lower intensity.

= = = Breakthrough and first Olympics (2013–2014) = = =

The 2013–14 season marked Halvorsen-Maaby's breakthrough at senior level. She achieved her first World Cup podium in December 2013, finishing third in the 10 kilometre classic in Lillehammer behind two compatriots. She went on to record three further podium finishes before the Olympic break and was selected for the Norwegian team for the 2014 Winter Olympics in Sochi, Russia.

At Sochi, she competed in three events. She finished ninth in the 10 kilometre classic and twelfth in the 15 kilometre skiathlon. In the 4 × 5 kilometre relay, she skied the second (classic) leg for Norway and handed over in second position; the team ultimately finished with the bronze medal after a close sprint finish for second place. It was her first Olympic medal. She did not start the 30 kilometre freestyle, having been withdrawn by team management in favour of a skier with stronger skating results.

She finished the 2013–14 World Cup season ranked 11th overall and fourth in the distance standings.

= = = World Championship success (2015–2017) = = =

At the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships 2015 in Falun, Sweden, Halvorsen-Maaby won her first senior world championship medals: a silver in the 10 kilometre classic and gold in the 4 × 5 kilometre relay. She also finished fifth in the 30 kilometre classic mass start, a race held in heavy snowfall in which she had led for much of the second half before fading in the final climb.

During the 2015–16 season she won her first individual World Cup race, a 10 kilometre classic interval start in Toblach, Italy, in January 2016. She added two further victories that season, in Nové Město and Lahti, and finished the season third overall in the World Cup standings, the highest ranking of her career up to that point. She also finished fourth in the Tour de Ski, losing the podium position on the final climb of the Alpe Cermis.

At the 2017 World Championships in Lahti, Finland, she won bronze in the 15 kilometre skiathlon and gold in the 30 kilometre classic mass start, the latter by a margin of 23.4 seconds after breaking away from the leading group with roughly eight kilometres remaining. The race was widely described in the Norwegian press as the most complete performance of her career to date. She also won a second relay gold with the Norwegian team.

= = = Olympic gold (2018) = = =

Halvorsen-Maaby entered the 2018 Winter Olympics in Pyeongchang, South Korea, as one of the favourites in the classic distance events. Her results in the early part of the Games were mixed: she finished sixth in the 15 kilometre skiathlon and fourth in the 10 kilometre freestyle, the latter being an event in which she had not been considered a medal contender.

In the 4 × 5 kilometre relay she again skied a classic leg and Norway won the silver medal, finishing behind Sweden.

The women's 30 kilometre classic mass start, held on the final day of the competition at the Alpensia Cross-Country Centre, was her final event of the Games. Skiing in temperatures of around −14 °C, she remained within a lead group of seven skiers through the first 15 kilometres before accelerating on the steepest section of the course on the fourth of six laps. She reached the finish in a time of 1:22:17.6, 41.2 seconds ahead of the silver medallist. The victory was Norway's second gold medal in the event in consecutive Games.

Following the Olympics, she was awarded the Egebergs Ærespris, a Norwegian honour given to athletes who have excelled in more than one sport, partly in recognition of her earlier national-level results in orienteering. She was also named Norwegian Sportswoman of the Year at the 2019 Idrettsgallaen.

= = = Tour de Ski title and later cross-country career (2018–2022) = = =

The 2018–19 season was affected by a respiratory illness that caused her to miss most of December and the start of the Tour de Ski. She returned in time for the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships 2019 in Seefeld, Austria, where she won silver in the 30 kilometre freestyle mass start, one of her few major medals in skating technique.

In the 2019–20 season, Halvorsen-Maaby won the overall title in the Tour de Ski, taking the leader's bib after the fourth stage in Lenzerheide and holding it through the final climb to the top of the Alpe Cermis in Val di Fiemme. Her overall winning margin was 38 seconds. She also won two individual stages during the event. The season was cut short in March 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic, and she finished second overall in the World Cup.

At the 2021 World Championships in Oberstdorf, Germany, she won gold in the 10 kilometre classic and silver in the relay, bringing her total of World Championship medals to ten at that stage. Her 10 kilometre victory came in rain and wet snow conditions which several competitors described as among the most difficult they had encountered in an international race.

She was selected for her third Olympic Games in 2022 in Beijing, China. She finished fifth in the 10 kilometre classic and seventh in the 30 kilometre freestyle, the latter shortened due to wind and low temperatures. In the relay, Norway won bronze, giving her a fourth Olympic medal. She later said that she had struggled with the limited preparation time at altitude and that the Beijing Games had been "the least enjoyable sporting experience" of her career.

= = = Transition to biathlon (2022–2026) = = =

In May 2022, Halvorsen-Maaby announced that she would leave the national cross-country team and attempt to qualify for the Norwegian biathlon programme. The decision attracted considerable attention in Norway, where few elite cross-country skiers had made such a switch at a comparable age and level of achievement. She cited a desire for "a new challenge that would keep me motivated" and noted that she had practised recreational shooting with her father as a teenager.

She trained with the Norwegian Biathlon Federation's development group during the 2022–23 season, working with shooting coach Siegfried Mazet's successor in the programme, and competed primarily in the IBU Cup. Her early results were characterised by fast ski times combined with low shooting accuracy; in her first IBU Cup season she hit approximately 74 percent of her targets, compared with a typical average above 85 percent among top-level World Cup competitors.

She made her Biathlon World Cup debut in December 2023 in Hochfilzen, Austria, finishing 41st in the sprint. Her shooting improved steadily through the 2023–24 season, and in March 2024 she achieved her first World Cup top-ten result with an eighth place in the mass start at Oslo Holmenkollen, where she was cheered by a large home crowd.

At the Biathlon World Championships 2025 in Lenzerheide, Switzerland, she was a member of the Norwegian women's relay team that won the bronze medal, her first and only international championship medal in biathlon. In doing so she became one of a small number of athletes to have won world championship medals in both cross-country skiing and biathlon.

She failed to gain selection for the biathlon squad for the 2026 Winter Olympics in Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, finishing fifth in the internal Norwegian selection ranking, where only four places were available. She announced her retirement from competitive sport in April 2026, shortly after her 35th birthday.

= = Skiing style = =

Halvorsen-Maaby was widely regarded as a specialist in classic technique, particularly on courses with long, steep climbs. Coaches and commentators frequently highlighted her efficient diagonal stride and her ability to maintain a high stride frequency late in races. Former national team coach Ole Morten Iversen described her as "a skier who gets stronger as the race gets harder".

Her sprint ability was comparatively limited; she rarely advanced beyond the quarter-finals in individual sprint events and never reached a World Cup sprint final. As a result, her success came mainly in interval-start and mass-start distance races, as well as in multi-stage events such as the Tour de Ski, where consistency over several days was rewarded.

Her training philosophy emphasised a high volume of low-intensity work. In a 2020 interview with the newspaper Aftenposten, she reported that she trained approximately 850 hours per year at the peak of her career, of which more than 85 percent was at low intensity.

= = Personal life = =

Halvorsen-Maaby married Eirik Solstad, a physiotherapist who worked with the Norwegian cross-country team, in 2019. The couple have one daughter, born in 2023, and live in Lillehammer. She returned to competition approximately six months after giving birth, during her biathlon career.

She holds a bachelor's degree in sport science from the Norwegian School of Sport Sciences, which she completed part-time between 2012 and 2018. Since her retirement, she has worked as an expert commentator for the Norwegian broadcaster NRK and as a youth development consultant for the Norwegian Ski Federation.

She has been involved in campaigns promoting physical activity among girls in rural areas, and in 2021 she co-founded a small foundation in Tynset that provides equipment grants to young skiers from low-income families. She has also spoken publicly about the pressures of elite sport, including a 2022 period of overtraining syndrome that she described in her 2024 autobiography, Lange bakker ("Long Hills").

= = Results summary = =

= = = Olympic Games = = =

Halvorsen-Maaby won four Olympic medals: one gold, one silver and two bronze.

2014 Sochi: Bronze, 4 × 5 km relay
2018 Pyeongchang: Gold, 30 km classic mass start; Silver, 4 × 5 km relay
2022 Beijing: Bronze, 4 × 5 km relay

= = = World Championships = = =

She won eleven medals at the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships (five gold, four silver and two bronze) and one bronze medal at the Biathlon World Championships.

2013 Val di Fiemme: Bronze, 4 × 5 km relay
2015 Falun: Gold, 4 × 5 km relay; Silver, 10 km classic
2017 Lahti: Gold, 30 km classic mass start; Gold, 4 × 5 km relay; Bronze, 15 km skiathlon
2019 Seefeld: Silver, 30 km freestyle mass start; Gold, 4 × 5 km relay
2021 Oberstdorf: Gold, 10 km classic; Silver, 4 × 5 km relay
2023 Planica: Silver, 4 × 5 km relay (named in squad; skied first leg)
2025 Lenzerheide (biathlon): Bronze, 4 × 6 km relay

= = = World Cup = = =

Over her cross-country career she recorded 14 individual World Cup victories and 41 individual podium finishes. Her best overall World Cup ranking was second, achieved in 2019–20. She won the Tour de Ski once, in 2019–20, and finished on the podium of the event on three other occasions.

In biathlon she competed in 46 World Cup races without recording an individual podium, with a best individual result of fifth in the pursuit at Antholz-Anterselva in January 2025.
