Mirela Vântu

Mirela Vântu (born 14 March 1991) is a Romanian retired rower who competed primarily in the women's single sculls and double sculls. She represented Romania at three Summer Olympic Games, winning a silver medal in the double sculls at the 2016 Summer Olympics in Rio de Janeiro and a bronze medal in the single sculls at the 2020 Summer Olympics in Tokyo. She was also a two-time World Rowing Championships medallist and won four titles at the European Rowing Championships. After retiring from international competition in 2023, she became a coach with the Romanian Rowing Federation's junior programme.

= = Early life = =

Vântu was born in Tulcea, a port city on the Danube in eastern Romania, to Gheorghe Vântu, a fisheries technician, and Elena Vântu, a primary school teacher. She was the second of three children. Growing up near the Danube Delta, she spent much of her childhood on or near the water, and she later said in interviews that she had learned to handle a small wooden boat before she learned to ride a bicycle.

She initially took up swimming at the age of eight at a municipal pool in Tulcea, where she trained for four years without notable success at the national level. In 2003, a rowing coach from the CSS Tulcea sports school, Ion Dumitrescu, noticed her height and arm span during a school physical education assessment and invited her to try rowing. At twelve she was already 1.74 metres tall, and she grew to 1.85 metres by the age of seventeen.

Vântu attended the Grigore Moisil Theoretical High School in Tulcea and continued training with Dumitrescu throughout her secondary education. She later studied physical education and sport at Ovidius University of Constanța, graduating in 2015 with a degree completed largely through distance arrangements made for national team athletes.

= = Junior career = =

Vântu competed in her first national junior championships in 2006, finishing fifth in the junior single sculls. The following year she won the national junior title in the same event, which led to her selection for the national junior training group based at Snagov Lake, north of Bucharest.

At the 2008 World Rowing Junior Championships in Linz, Austria, she was part of the Romanian junior women's quadruple sculls that finished fourth, missing a medal by 0.71 seconds. In 2009 she returned to the single sculls and won the silver medal at the World Rowing Junior Championships in Brive-la-Gaillarde, France, finishing behind a German rower by just over two seconds. Coaches with the national federation noted her strong finishing pace, which would become a characteristic feature of her racing at senior level.

= = Senior career = =

= = = Early senior years (2010–2012) = = =

Vântu moved to the under-23 category in 2010 and won a bronze medal in the single sculls at the World Rowing U23 Championships in Brest, Belarus. In 2011 she made her senior international debut at a World Rowing Cup regatta in Munich, racing in the women's double sculls with Ana-Maria Ciobanu. The pair finished sixth in the A final.

During this period the Romanian women's programme was focused heavily on the eight, and Vântu was briefly trialled in sweep rowing in the spring of 2011. Federation coaches ultimately judged her better suited to sculling, and she remained in the sculling group under head coach Valeriu Popa.

Vântu was selected for the 2012 Summer Olympics in London as a member of the women's quadruple sculls. The crew finished fourth in their heat and second in the repechage, qualifying for the final, where they placed sixth. Vântu, then 21, was the youngest member of the crew. Although the result was below the federation's expectations, her performance in training camps that summer led to her being identified as a potential small-boat specialist for the next Olympic cycle.

= = = Double sculls partnership (2013–2016) = = =

In 2013, Vântu was paired with the more experienced Roxana Iliescu in the women's double sculls. Iliescu, who was five years older and had previously rowed in the Romanian eight, occupied the stroke seat, with Vântu in bow. The partnership produced immediate results: the pair won the gold medal at the 2013 European Rowing Championships in Seville, Spain, and finished fifth at the 2013 World Rowing Championships in Chungju, South Korea.

The following season they retained their European title in Belgrade and won their first World Rowing Cup race in Lucerne. At the 2014 World Rowing Championships in Amsterdam they took the bronze medal, finishing behind crews from New Zealand and Great Britain. It was the first world championship medal in the women's double sculls for Romania since 2001.

The 2015 season was disrupted when Vântu suffered a stress fracture of a rib in April, a common injury among rowers. She missed the first two World Cup regattas and returned to racing only at the European Championships in Poznań, where the pair finished fourth. At the 2015 World Rowing Championships in Aiguebelette, France, they placed fourth in the final, securing an Olympic quota place for Romania in the event.

At the 2016 Summer Olympics in Rio de Janeiro, Vântu and Iliescu won their heat and semi-final at the Lagoa Rodrigo de Freitas. Conditions on the day of the final were windy, and several races in the regatta had been postponed earlier in the week. In the final, the Romanian crew were in fifth place at the 1,000-metre mark but moved through the field in the second half of the race, finishing second behind Poland and ahead of Lithuania. The silver medal was Romania's first Olympic medal in the women's double sculls. Both rowers were awarded the Order of Sporting Merit, Third Class, by the President of Romania later that year.

= = = Single sculls (2017–2021) = = =

Iliescu announced her retirement from international competition at the end of 2016, and Vântu moved into the single sculls for the 2017 season. Her transition was gradual; she finished seventh at the 2017 World Rowing Championships in Sarasota, Florida, and spent the following winter working with a biomechanics team from the National Institute for Sport Research in Bucharest to adjust her technique to the lighter, less stable boat.

In 2018 she won the single sculls at the European Rowing Championships in Glasgow, her third European title and her first in an individual event. She went on to win the silver medal at the 2018 World Rowing Championships in Plovdiv, Bulgaria, finishing 1.14 seconds behind the winner. Commentators noted the race as one of the closest women's single sculls finals of the decade.

At the 2019 World Rowing Championships in Linz-Ottensheim, Austria, Vântu finished fifth, which was sufficient to qualify a boat for Romania for the 2020 Olympics. She had been affected by a viral illness in the weeks before the championships and later described the season as the most difficult of her career.

Following the postponement of the 2020 Summer Olympics because of the COVID-19 pandemic, Vântu trained largely alone at Snagov during the spring of 2020, using an ergometer and a single scull while group training was restricted. She won her fourth European title at the 2021 European Rowing Championships in Varese, Italy.

At the Tokyo Olympics, held in 2021 at the Sea Forest Waterway, Vântu won her quarter-final and finished second in her semi-final. In the final she was in fourth position for most of the race before overtaking a Swiss rower in the final 250 metres to take the bronze medal. Her time of 7:16.48 was a personal best in Olympic competition. She became the first Romanian woman to win an Olympic medal in the single sculls since the 1980s.

= = = Final seasons and retirement (2022–2023) = = =

Vântu continued competing in 2022, but a recurrence of lower back problems limited her training. She finished fourth at the 2022 European Rowing Championships in Munich and did not compete at the World Championships that year. In early 2023 she returned to the double sculls with the younger rower Ioana Marinescu, with the aim of qualifying for the 2024 Summer Olympics in Paris. The pair finished ninth at the 2023 World Rowing Championships in Belgrade, missing direct Olympic qualification.

In November 2023, Vântu announced her retirement from international rowing, citing her ongoing back condition and a desire to begin coaching. She did not take part in the final Olympic qualification regatta in 2024.

= = Rowing style = =

Vântu was regarded as a strong finisher who often raced conservatively in the first half of a 2,000-metre race before increasing her stroke rate in the final 500 metres. Analysts at World Rowing noted that her average split over the last quarter of her races was frequently among the fastest in the field. Her coaches attributed this to her endurance base, developed partly through long-distance training on the Danube during her youth.

Her technique was described as long and relatively low-rated, with a pronounced emphasis on leg drive. In the single sculls she typically raced at a lower stroke rate than many of her rivals, a feature that Romanian coaches linked to her height and reach. Critics occasionally pointed to slow starts as a weakness, and several of her defeats came in races where she was unable to recover from a deficit in the opening 500 metres.

= = Coaching career = =

After retiring, Vântu completed a coaching qualification through the Romanian Rowing Federation and in 2024 was appointed assistant coach of the national junior women's sculling group. In this role she has worked mainly with athletes aged 16 to 18 at the federation's base at Snagov. She has also been involved in a regional talent identification programme in Tulcea County, modelled on the school screening through which she herself was introduced to the sport.

In 2025 she was elected to the athletes' commission of the Romanian Olympic and Sports Committee, where she has advocated for improved post-career support for retired athletes.

= = Personal life = =

Vântu married Andrei Rădulescu, a former canoeist, in 2019. The couple have one daughter, born in 2024, and live in Bucharest. She has spoken publicly about the challenges of managing chronic back pain and has taken part in campaigns by Romanian sports medicine organisations to raise awareness of injury prevention in young athletes.

Outside rowing, she has an interest in the ecology of the Danube Delta and has supported conservation projects in the region. In 2022 she served as an ambassador for a regional campaign promoting clean waterways.

= = Legacy = =

Vântu is considered one of the most successful Romanian scullers of her generation. Her Olympic medals in 2016 and 2021 came at a time when Romanian rowing results were declining from the levels achieved in the 1990s and early 2000s, when the country's women's eight dominated international competition. Her success in small boats was cited by the federation as evidence that Romania could compete in sculling events as well as in sweep rowing, and the number of junior girls registered in sculling programmes in Romania rose during the late 2010s.

In 2022 the rowing base of CSS Tulcea renamed its boathouse in her honour.
