Lights of the Post Office

Lights of the Post Office (Japanese: 郵便局のあかり, Hepburn: Yūbinkyoku no Akari) is a Japanese television drama series that aired on TBS Television from January 11 to March 15, 2019, in the network's Friday 22:00 time slot. The series ran for ten episodes. It stars Haruka Morinaga as Sayo Kitajima, a former Tokyo bank employee who takes over the management of a small post office in a declining fishing town in Iwate Prefecture after her estranged grandfather falls ill. The screenplay was written by Mitsuko Hanazawa, and the series was directed by Takuya Onodera, Ren Ishiguro and Emi Satake.

The drama was noted for its depiction of rural depopulation in the Tōhoku region and for its use of location filming in coastal communities affected by the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami. Its average household rating in the Kantō region was 10.4 percent, according to Video Research. The series won the Best Drama award at the 101st Television Drama Academy Awards. A two-hour television special followed in December 2020.

= = Plot = =

Sayo Kitajima is a 32-year-old loan officer at a major Tokyo bank. She is transferred to a back-office department after she refuses to pressure an elderly client into buying a high-risk investment product. Soon afterward she receives a telephone call telling her that her grandfather, Genzō Kitajima, has collapsed. Genzō is the longtime postmaster of the Hamanoura Post Office in the fictional town of Hamanoura on the Sanriku coast. Sayo has not visited him in fifteen years, since a family dispute that followed her mother's death.

Sayo travels to Hamanoura intending to stay only a few days. She finds that the post office is the last remaining public service counter in the town. The local branch of the agricultural cooperative closed several years earlier, and the nearest bank is forty minutes away by car. Genzō has been delivering pensions, reading letters aloud to residents with poor eyesight, and informally checking on elderly people who live alone. His doctors advise that he cannot return to work for at least six months, and the regional office of Japan Post begins to consider closing the branch.

Sayo agrees to act as temporary manager until a decision is made. She is reluctant at first and approaches the job through numbers and efficiency, which brings her into conflict with the office's two remaining staff members. Kōhei Yamaguchi is a quiet mail carrier in his forties who returned to Hamanoura after the 2011 tsunami. Michiru Abe is a part-time counter clerk and single mother. Through her contact with residents, including a retired fisherman who writes letters every week to a son who never replies and a high school student hoping to win a scholarship to study in Sendai, Sayo gradually comes to understand the post office's role in the community.

The second half of the series centers on the regional office's review of the branch and on Sayo's attempts to keep it open. She organizes a delivery service for medicine and groceries and helps residents sell local products by mail order. Sayo also learns the reason for the rift between her mother and Genzō. Years earlier, Genzō had concealed a letter that her father sent before he left the family. In the final episode, the regional office decides to keep the Hamanoura branch open on a trial basis for three years. Sayo resigns from her bank and stays in the town as postmaster. Genzō dies peacefully in the spring, and the series ends with Sayo delivering the first letter the retired fisherman's son has sent in many years.

= = Production = =

= = = Development = = =

The series was conceived by TBS producer Naoko Fujisaki, who had worked on several of the network's family dramas in the 2010s. According to Fujisaki, the idea came from a 2016 newspaper article about a post office in rural Akita Prefecture whose staff had noticed that an elderly resident had stopped collecting his mail and found him injured at home. Fujisaki wanted to make a drama about "the small infrastructure that holds a town together." She approached screenwriter Mitsuko Hanazawa, who was known for her work on single-episode dramas for NHK and had not previously written a serialized drama for a commercial network.

Hanazawa spent about four months in 2017 researching the project. She interviewed postal workers in Iwate, Miyagi and Fukushima prefectures and stayed for several weeks in a fishing community in southern Iwate. In a 2019 interview with the magazine Drama Monthly, she said she had deliberately avoided making the 2011 disaster the main subject of the story. "The people I met did not want to be defined only by the tsunami," she said. "It is part of their lives, but so are school entrance exams, festivals and arguments with their neighbors." The town of Hamanoura is a composite of several communities, and the name was chosen because it did not match any existing municipality.

Japan Post Holdings provided consultation on postal procedures and allowed the production to use authentic uniforms, signage and equipment. The company stated that it did not have editorial control over the scripts. Some plot elements, including the regional office's review of the branch, were reportedly discussed with the company at length before filming began.

= = = Casting = = =

The casting of Haruka Morinaga in the lead role was announced on October 15, 2018. It was her first leading role in a prime-time drama on TBS. Morinaga had previously been known mainly for supporting roles in film. Fujisaki said she had been chosen because she could portray "a person who is capable but tired, who has forgotten how to be kind to herself." Shōzō Nakadai, a stage actor who rarely appeared on television, was cast as Genzō after Hanazawa said she had written the character with him in mind. Ken'ichi Tsuruoka, who was born in Miyagi Prefecture, said in a press conference that he had accepted the role partly because of the series' setting.

Several minor roles were filled by residents of the towns where filming took place. Members of a local fishermen's cooperative appeared as themselves in the third and seventh episodes.

= = = Filming = = =

Principal photography began in November 2018 and ended in early March 2019, shortly before the final episode aired. Exterior scenes were filmed mainly in Ōfunato and Kamaishi in Iwate Prefecture, with additional location work in Kesennuma, Miyagi Prefecture. The production chose not to film at an operating post office. It built the exterior of the Hamanoura Post Office on vacant land near a harbor, using materials salvaged from a closed public building. The interior was built at TBS's Midoriyama Studio in Yokohama.

Winter weather caused several delays. A heavy snowfall in January 2019 forced the cancellation of three days of location filming, and director Takuya Onodera later said the production had rewritten a scene in episode 6 to make use of the snow. The set was dismantled after filming ended. Its mailbox was donated to the city of Ōfunato and installed near a local tourist information center.

= = = Music = = =

The score was composed by Yūko Senba and recorded with a small string ensemble and piano. The theme song, "Tegami no Tsuzuki" (手紙の続き, "The Rest of the Letter"), was performed by the folk-pop singer Aoi Takamine. It was released as a single by Pony Canyon on February 6, 2019. The single reached number 9 on the Oricon weekly singles chart and was certified gold by the Recording Industry Association of Japan for digital downloads in 2020. An original soundtrack album was released on March 6, 2019.

= = Broadcast = =

Lights of the Post Office premiered on January 11, 2019, with an extended 70-minute first episode. The remaining episodes ran for 54 minutes. The final episode, broadcast on March 15, 2019, was extended to 64 minutes. The series was streamed on the Paravi service in Japan after each broadcast. It was later made available internationally with English, Traditional Chinese and Korean subtitles.

The series was broadcast in Taiwan by a cable channel beginning in September 2019 and in Hong Kong in 2020. A Blu-ray and DVD box set was released by TC Entertainment on July 24, 2019. It included a making-of documentary, cast interviews and a short spin-off episode focusing on Michiru Abe.

= = = Special = = =

A two-hour special, Lights of the Post Office: The Winter Delivery (郵便局のあかり 冬の配達), aired on December 18, 2020. Set about a year and a half after the end of the series, it follows Sayo as the post office adapts to restrictions during the COVID-19 pandemic and as Natsuki returns from university in Sendai. Hanazawa wrote the script, and most of the main cast returned. The special recorded a household rating of 11.2 percent in the Kantō region.

= = Reception = =

= = = Ratings = = =

The first episode recorded an average household rating of 11.8 percent in the Kantō region, according to Video Research. Ratings dipped to 8.9 percent for the fifth episode, which aired against a live broadcast of an international football match. They recovered in the second half of the series, and the final episode recorded the series high of 12.6 percent. The average across all ten episodes was 10.4 percent. Ratings were higher in the Tōhoku region. The final episode recorded 17.3 percent on TBS's affiliate stations there.

= = = Critical response = = =

The series received generally positive reviews from Japanese critics. Writing for the Asahi Shimbun, television columnist Hideo Ikegami praised Hanazawa's script for "resisting easy tears" and described Nakadai's performance as "the quiet center of the drama." The weekly magazine Shūkan Bunshun included the series in its list of the best dramas of the winter 2019 season. It singled out the fourth episode, in which a fifty-year-old letter is delivered, as one of the strongest single episodes of the year.

Morinaga's performance was widely praised. Several reviewers noted her restraint in the early episodes, when Sayo is portrayed as emotionally closed off. Some critics, however, found the series' middle section slow. The film critic Masaru Fukuda wrote that the subplot involving Sayo's former colleague Satoshi Takeda "adds little and distracts from the town." Others argued that the regional office's decision in the final episode was too convenient. One reviewer called it "a fairy-tale ending in a drama that had otherwise taken care to be realistic."

The depiction of the disaster-affected region was the subject of particular attention. A number of commentators welcomed the decision to show the communities as places with an ongoing everyday life rather than solely as sites of tragedy. The Iwate Nippō newspaper reported that residents of Ōfunato had generally responded favorably. It quoted a local fisherman who said the drama had "shown that we are still here." A smaller number of viewers criticized the series for presenting the economic problems of rural depopulation in an overly hopeful way.

= = = Social impact = = =

During the broadcast period, the series was credited with renewed public interest in rural post offices. In February 2019, Japan Post reported an increase in requests for its "watch-over" service, in which mail carriers check on elderly residents for a fee. The company acknowledged that the drama may have contributed to the rise. The number of letters sent through the post office in Ōfunato also increased during the spring of 2019, as fans of the series mailed postcards from the city.

The city of Ōfunato published a map of filming locations, and local tourism officials estimated that about 30,000 additional visitors came to the area in 2019 as a result of the drama. The donated mailbox from the set became a popular photo spot. In 2021 the city began a program in which letters posted in the mailbox are given a special commemorative postmark.

= = Accolades = =

At the 101st Television Drama Academy Awards, held in spring 2019, Lights of the Post Office won Best Drama. Haruka Morinaga won Best Actress, Shōzō Nakadai won Best Supporting Actor, and Mitsuko Hanazawa won Best Screenplay. The theme song, "Tegami no Tsuzuki," was nominated for Best Theme Song but lost.

The series also received the Excellence Award in the television drama category at the 2019 Galaxy Awards, presented by the Japan Council for Better Radio and Television. Hanazawa received the Mukōda Kuniko Award nomination for her screenplay, though the prize that year went to another writer. At the Tokyo Drama Awards 2019, the series won the Local Drama Special Prize, which recognizes productions that portray regional Japan.

= = Legacy = =

Lights of the Post Office has been cited as part of a wave of Japanese television dramas in the late 2010s and early 2020s that focused on rural settings and community institutions rather than on urban workplaces. In a 2022 retrospective, the trade publication Nikkei Entertainment described it as one of the dramas that "moved the center of the Friday night drama away from Tokyo." Hanazawa went on to write two further serialized dramas for TBS, both set outside major cities.

The series was adapted into a novel by Hanazawa, published by Gentōsha in June 2019, which expanded several subplots and added a chapter told from Genzō's perspective. A stage adaptation produced by a Sendai-based theater company toured the Tōhoku region in 2022, with local actors in most roles.

In 2023, the production company that handled the series' international distribution announced that the format had been licensed for a South Korean remake. The remake was reported to be in development for a streaming service as of 2025, with the setting moved to a fishing village in South Jeolla Province.
