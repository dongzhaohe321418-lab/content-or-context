The Lantern Watch

The Lantern Watch is an American comic book series created by writer Delia Marchetti and artist Owen Szabo, first published by Halcyon Comics in March 1971. The series follows a rotating group of costumed vigilantes who operate from a decommissioned lighthouse on the coast of the fictional city of Port Averil, Massachusetts. The original series ran for 214 issues until 1989 and has since been relaunched four times, most recently in 2022.

The series is noted for its early use of continuing storylines across multiple issues, its ensemble cast drawn from working-class backgrounds, and its recurring theme of inherited responsibility. The title character role, known as "the Keeper," has been held by six different characters over the course of the series' publication history. Critics have cited *The Lantern Watch* as an influence on the team books of the 1980s, and the series received three Shazam Awards and two Eisner Award nominations during its various runs.

= = Publication history = =

= = = Origins (1969–1971) = = =

Delia Marchetti had worked as an assistant editor at Halcyon Comics since 1966, mainly on the company's romance and western anthologies. In interviews given in the 1990s, Marchetti said the idea for *The Lantern Watch* grew out of a summer spent in Gloucester, Massachusetts, where she met the family of a retired lighthouse keeper whose duties had passed from father to son over three generations. She later described the concept as "a superhero job that you inherit like a family business, whether you want it or not."

Marchetti pitched the series to Halcyon editor-in-chief Harold Brisk in late 1969. Brisk initially rejected it, reportedly considering a coastal setting too regional for a national audience. The proposal was approved the following year after Halcyon's science fiction title *Starward* was cancelled, leaving an open slot in the publishing schedule. Owen Szabo, a staff artist who had previously drawn war comics, was assigned to the book. Szabo designed the original Keeper costume, a dark green bodysuit with a brass-colored lens over the chest, based on the Fresnel lenses used in lighthouse lamps.

= = = Original series (1971–1989) = = =

*The Lantern Watch* #1 was released with a cover date of March 1971. The first issue introduced Thomas Ashgrove, a dockworker who learns that his late grandfather had been the first Keeper, a guardian whose power derived from a mysterious light housed in the Saltmarsh Point lighthouse. Sales of the early issues were modest, and the book was published bimonthly until 1973, when it moved to a monthly schedule.

Marchetti wrote the first 58 issues. Her tenure established most of the series' core supporting cast, including the harbormaster Ruth Calloway, the lighthouse's caretaker and historian Abel Penhallow, and the antagonist known as the Undertow. Szabo remained on art until issue #41, after which he was succeeded by Ricardo Ventura, whose more angular, heavily inked style became closely associated with the book during the mid-1970s.

Issue #50 (May 1976) is generally regarded as a turning point for the series. In a storyline titled "The Long Dark," Thomas Ashgrove was killed, and the role of Keeper passed to his younger sister, Marion Ashgrove. The death of a title character was uncommon in mainstream comics at the time, and the issue drew considerable reader mail, much of it hostile. Halcyon's sales records, later published in the trade magazine *Panel Ledger*, show that circulation rose by approximately 30 percent in the following year.

Marchetti left the series in 1977 to become an editor at Halcyon. She was replaced by writer Gerald Okafor, who wrote issues #59 through #121. Okafor expanded the series' scope beyond Port Averil, introducing a network of other lighthouses along the Atlantic coast, each with its own Keeper. This concept, referred to in the comics as "the Chain," became a fixture of later volumes. Okafor also introduced the series' first major crossover, "Fog Season" (1980), which ran through *The Lantern Watch* and two other Halcyon titles.

The final decade of the original series was marked by frequent creative turnover. Between 1983 and 1989, the book had seven different writers and eleven pencillers. Sales declined through the late 1980s, and Halcyon cancelled the series with issue #214 (August 1989). The final issue, written by Marchetti as a guest, depicted the lighthouse being sealed and its light extinguished.

= = = Volume 2 (1993–1998) = = =

Halcyon relaunched the series in April 1993 under the title *Lantern Watch*, without the definite article. The second volume was written by Priya Ramaswamy and drawn by Calvin Breck. Set fifteen years after the original series, it introduced Daniel Ferrante, a former Coast Guard officer who reopens the lighthouse after a series of shipwrecks. Ramaswamy's run was darker in tone than the original, reflecting broader trends in the industry during the 1990s, and it addressed subjects such as the decline of the New England fishing industry and opioid addiction in coastal towns.

Volume 2 received favorable reviews, and the issue #12 story "Ballast" was nominated for the Eisner Award for Best Single Issue in 1995. However, sales were affected by the contraction of the comics market in the mid-1990s, and the series was cancelled with issue #61 in 1998.

= = = Volume 3 (2004–2011) = = =

The third volume launched in 2004 as part of Halcyon's "Second Light" publishing initiative, which revived several of the company's dormant properties. Written initially by Marcus Hale and later by Joanna Whitlock, the third volume restored continuity with the original series and featured the return of Marion Ashgrove, now in her fifties, as a mentor to a new Keeper, the teenage Lucia Delgado. The relationship between the two characters was widely praised by reviewers, and Delgado became one of the most popular characters in the franchise.

Volume 3 lasted 84 issues and included the company-wide event "Low Tide" (2008), in which every lighthouse in the Chain was extinguished simultaneously. Whitlock's final storyline, "Beacon," concluded the volume in 2011 with Delgado relinquishing the role.

= = = Volume 4 and Volume 5 (2015–present) = = =

A fourth volume, published from 2015 to 2018, was written by Simon Achebe and featured an older Thomas Ashgrove, revealed to have survived the events of the 1976 storyline in a suspended state within the light itself. The return of the character was controversial among longtime readers, and the volume was cancelled after 30 issues.

The current, fifth volume began in September 2022, written by Hana Iwasaki with art by Leon Marquette. It features a reconstituted team of Keepers drawn from across the Chain and operates under a loose structure in which several characters share the title concurrently. As of 2026, the fifth volume has published 48 issues.

= = Fictional premise = =

= = = The Light = = =

The central element of the series is the Light, an energy source housed in the lantern room of the Saltmarsh Point lighthouse. Its origin has been revealed differently over the course of the series. In the original series, Abel Penhallow states that the Light was brought to the lighthouse in 1822 by an unknown sailor who survived a shipwreck. Volume 3 expanded this account, establishing that the Light is one of thirteen fragments of a single source scattered along the Atlantic coast, each of which became the basis for one lighthouse in the Chain.

The Light grants its bearer, the Keeper, a range of abilities while they remain within sight of the sea. These include enhanced strength and endurance, the ability to project beams of concentrated light, and a limited form of precognition referred to in the comics as "seeing the reef," which allows the Keeper to perceive imminent dangers. The powers diminish sharply when the Keeper travels inland, a limitation that writers have used frequently as a plot device.

The Light is depicted as semi-sentient. It selects its Keeper, usually but not always from among the descendants of previous holders, and it cannot be transferred voluntarily. A Keeper who abandons the lighthouse gradually loses their abilities, while the Light grows dim and the surrounding coast becomes vulnerable to disaster.

= = = Setting = = =

Port Averil is a fictional mid-sized port city located on the North Shore of Massachusetts. It is depicted as a declining industrial town with a large immigrant population, particularly of Portuguese, Irish, and Cape Verdean descent. Recurring locations include the Saltmarsh Point lighthouse, the Calloway Marine Supply store, St. Brendan's Church, and the abandoned Pell Cannery, which serves as a frequent battleground.

Marchetti stated that she deliberately avoided a large metropolitan setting, explaining that she wanted "a place where everyone knows the hero's family." The small-town quality of Port Averil, in which characters regularly encounter one another in daily life, is often cited as one of the series' distinguishing features.

= = Characters = =

= = = Keepers = = =

The following characters have held the role of Keeper of the Saltmarsh Point light:

* '''Thomas Ashgrove''' (1971–1976; 2015–2018) – A dockworker and Vietnam War veteran who inherits the role from his grandfather. He is portrayed as reluctant and short-tempered but deeply loyal to his community.
* '''Marion Ashgrove''' (1976–1989; mentor role 2004–2011) – Thomas's younger sister, a nursing student when she assumes the role. She is the longest-serving Keeper in the series and is generally considered its central figure.
* '''Daniel Ferrante''' (1993–1998) – A former Coast Guard officer with no family connection to the Light, whose selection is treated within the story as an anomaly.
* '''Lucia Delgado''' (2004–2011) – A sixteen-year-old high school student and the granddaughter of a Cape Verdean fisherman. She is the youngest Keeper in the series.
* '''Ezra Penhallow''' (2011; 2022–present) – The grandson of Abel Penhallow, who held the role briefly at the end of Volume 3 and returned as one of several concurrent Keepers in Volume 5.
* '''Nadia Okoro''' (2022–present) – A marine biologist who becomes the first Keeper to hold the title jointly with another character.

= = = Supporting characters = = =

* '''Abel Penhallow''' – The elderly caretaker of the lighthouse and an authority on its history. He served as the series' principal source of exposition until his death in Volume 2.
* '''Ruth Calloway''' – The harbormaster of Port Averil and a close ally of the Ashgrove family. She appears throughout all five volumes.
* '''Father Michael Doyle''' – The parish priest at St. Brendan's, who becomes aware of the Keeper's identity in the original series.
* '''Detective Rosa Almeida''' – A Port Averil police detective introduced in Volume 2 who initially pursues the Keeper as a vigilante before becoming an ally.

= = = Antagonists = = =

* '''The Undertow''' – The series' principal antagonist, a being composed of seawater and drowned memories who seeks to extinguish the Light. The character has been depicted with several different origins; most writers have treated it as a personification of the sea's destructive aspects.
* '''Captain Josiah Wren''' – The ghost of a nineteenth-century wrecker who lured ships onto rocks with false lights. He is portrayed as the Keeper's opposite and appears in every volume.
* '''Mercy Crane''' – A real-estate developer who seeks to purchase and demolish the lighthouse. Introduced in Volume 3, she represents the economic pressures facing the town rather than a supernatural threat.
* '''The Fogbound''' – A cult that worships the Undertow and recruits from among the desperate and unemployed in Port Averil.

= = Themes = =

Critics and scholars have identified several recurring themes in *The Lantern Watch*. The most frequently discussed is inherited obligation. Unlike many superhero narratives, in which the protagonist chooses to take up the role, the Keepers of the series generally receive their powers involuntarily and often resent them. Literary critic Margaret Esposito, in her 2009 study *Four-Color Coastlines*, argued that the series "treats heroism not as a calling but as a chore handed down at a deathbed."

The series has also been noted for its attention to economic decline. The deterioration of Port Averil's fishing and manufacturing industries forms a persistent background to the stories, and several antagonists, particularly Mercy Crane and the Fogbound, are depicted as exploiting the town's poverty. Writer Priya Ramaswamy described her run as "a superhero book about a town that is dying slowly, where the hero can stop a monster but not a factory closure."

Another recurring theme is the tension between individual and community. Because the Light is tied to a single place, the Keepers cannot easily leave Port Averil, and several storylines concern characters who must give up personal ambitions, such as university education or careers elsewhere, to remain in their role.

= = Reception = =

*The Lantern Watch* received generally positive critical attention during its original run, although it was not among Halcyon's best-selling titles. The death of Thomas Ashgrove in issue #50 was widely discussed in fan publications, and the comics historian Leonard Fisk later described it as "one of the first times a mainstream superhero book took its own stakes seriously."

The original series received Shazam Awards in 1976 and 1977 for Best Continuing Feature and in 1978 for Best Individual Story. Volume 2 and Volume 3 each received one Eisner Award nomination. Volume 3, particularly Joanna Whitlock's run, was frequently included in critics' year-end lists, and the collected edition *The Lantern Watch: Beacon* appeared on the *Library Journal* list of recommended graphic novels in 2012.

Reception of Volume 4 was more mixed. Reviewers criticized the return of Thomas Ashgrove as undercutting the significance of his earlier death, and several longtime readers organized a letter-writing campaign protesting the decision. Volume 5 has received favorable reviews, with critics praising Hana Iwasaki's use of an ensemble structure and Leon Marquette's artwork.

= = Collected editions = =

Halcyon Comics has reprinted the series in several formats. The original series was collected in a nine-volume *Lantern Watch Archives* hardcover set published between 2006 and 2014. Volume 2 was collected in four trade paperbacks, and Volume 3 was collected in seven. In 2021, Halcyon published *The Lantern Watch Omnibus*, a 1,200-page hardcover reprinting issues #1–#58 of the original series, corresponding to Delia Marchetti's tenure as writer.

= = In other media = =

= = = Television = = =

An animated adaptation, *Lantern Watch*, aired on a cable network for one season of 13 episodes in 1997. The series was based primarily on Volume 2 but featured Marion Ashgrove as the protagonist. It received modest ratings and was not renewed.

In 2019, a live-action television series was announced as being in development, with a pilot script written by Joanna Whitlock. As of 2026, the project has not entered production.

= = = Other media = =

A radio drama based on the "Long Dark" storyline was produced by a public radio station in Boston in 1984. A tabletop role-playing game supplement, *Keepers of the Chain*, was published in 2010. Lucia Delgado appeared as a playable character in a 2016 mobile game featuring characters from across Halcyon's catalogue.

= = Legacy = =

*The Lantern Watch* has been cited as an influence by several comics creators. Its model of a superhero title passed among successive holders has been compared to later legacy-based series, and its emphasis on a specific, economically depressed regional setting has been credited with encouraging similar approaches in independent comics during the 1990s. In 2011, the town of Gloucester, Massachusetts, hosted an exhibition of original artwork from the series at a local maritime museum, marking the series' fortieth anniversary.

Delia Marchetti was inducted into a comics industry hall of fame in 2003. She died in 2017. In her honor, Halcyon published a special one-shot, *The Lantern Watch: Last Light*, in 2018, featuring short stories by writers and artists from each volume of the series.
