Callum James Rennick

Callum James Rennick (born 14 March 1981) is a former New Zealand cricketer who played domestic first-class cricket for Otago and Canterbury, and represented New Zealand in nine Test matches and twenty-two One Day Internationals between 2005 and 2010. A left-handed top-order batsman who also bowled occasional right-arm medium pace, Rennick was known for his defensive technique and his ability to bat for long periods. He made 7,412 first-class runs at an average of 38.21, and scored a career-best 241 against Northern Districts at the University Oval in Dunedin in 2007. After retiring from playing in 2014, he coached at provincial level and later served as a national selector.

= = Early life = =

Rennick was born in Invercargill, Southland, the second of three children of Graham Rennick, a dairy farm contractor, and Helen Rennick (née Macpherson), a primary school teacher. The family moved to the rural township of Gore when he was four, and he grew up on a property outside the town. His father had played club cricket for the Mataura Valley side, and Rennick later said that he learned the game in the family's back paddock, batting against his older brother on a strip of rolled grass.

He attended Gore High School, where he played cricket and rugby union. As a teenager he played in the first XV as a fullback, but he gave up rugby at the age of sixteen after breaking his collarbone. In cricket he progressed through the Southland age-group sides and was selected for the Otago under-17 team in the 1997–98 season. In the national under-17 tournament that summer he scored 312 runs in five matches, including an unbeaten 128 against Auckland, which led to his selection for the New Zealand under-19 squad.

Rennick toured Sri Lanka with the New Zealand under-19 team in 1999 and played in the Under-19 Cricket World Cup in 2000, where he scored 184 runs in six innings. He was regarded by team management as a steady opening batsman rather than an aggressive stroke player, and he opened the batting in all of New Zealand's matches at the tournament.

After leaving school, Rennick moved to Dunedin to study physical education at the University of Otago. He played club cricket for the University club in the Dunedin senior competition and completed his degree in 2003, combining study with his early first-class career.

= = Domestic career = =

= = = Otago = = =

Rennick made his first-class debut for Otago against Wellington at the Basin Reserve in January 2001, at the age of nineteen. Batting at number three, he scored 14 and 37 in a drawn match. He played four further matches that season but struggled to establish himself, finishing with 156 runs at an average of 19.50.

The 2002–03 season brought his first first-class century, an innings of 112 against Central Districts at Molyneux Park in Alexandra. He batted for just under six hours, and the innings was noted in the local press for its patience on a slow pitch. He ended the season as Otago's second-highest run scorer in the State Championship, with 583 runs at 41.64.

He moved permanently to the role of opening batsman in the 2003–04 season, forming a partnership with the experienced right-hander Mark Tolley. The pair shared four century stands that season, including an opening partnership of 214 against Auckland at Eden Park Outer Oval, at the time Otago's highest first-wicket partnership against Auckland. Rennick scored 791 runs in the season, including three centuries, and was named Otago's player of the year.

His most productive season came in 2006–07, when he scored 1,043 first-class runs at an average of 61.35, becoming only the fourth Otago batsman to pass 1,000 runs in a domestic first-class season. This included his career-best score of 241 against Northern Districts at the University Oval in February 2007. Coming in to bat after Otago had lost an early wicket, he batted for nine hours and twenty minutes and faced 487 balls. The innings helped Otago to a first-innings total of 563 for 8 declared and an innings victory. He was named the domestic player of the season at the New Zealand Cricket awards that year.

Rennick was appointed Otago captain for the 2008–09 season, succeeding Tolley. His captaincy was generally regarded as cautious; under his leadership Otago finished third and then second in the Plunket Shield, but they did not win a title. He relinquished the captaincy at the end of the 2010–11 season, stating that he wished to concentrate on his batting.

= = = Canterbury = = =

In 2011 Rennick moved to Christchurch to join Canterbury, partly for family reasons and partly because of his frustration at his diminishing role in Otago's limited-overs sides. His move came months after the February 2011 Christchurch earthquake, and Canterbury played several of their home matches that season at venues outside the city while repairs were carried out.

He played three seasons for Canterbury, mainly batting in the middle order. In the 2012–13 season he scored 704 runs and was part of the Canterbury side that finished as runners-up in the Plunket Shield. He also captained the side in five matches when the regular captain was injured. His final first-class match was against Wellington at Hagley Oval in March 2014, in which he scored 58 in his last innings.

= = = Limited-overs and Twenty20 cricket = = =

Rennick was a regular member of Otago's one-day side for most of his career, usually batting in the top three. He was a member of the Otago team that reached the final of the State Shield in 2005–06, scoring 76 in the final, although Otago were defeated by Northern Districts. He was less successful in Twenty20 cricket, playing 28 domestic Twenty20 matches with a highest score of 52 and a strike rate of 104.6. By the end of his time at Otago he was seldom selected in the shortest format, which he cited as one reason for his move to Canterbury.

= = International career = =

= = = One Day International debut = = =

Rennick was first selected for New Zealand's One Day International squad for a home series against Sri Lanka in early 2005, following injuries to two senior batsmen. He made his debut at McLean Park in Napier, scoring 31 from 48 balls. He played in three matches of the series, with a top score of 44.

He was retained for a tour of Zimbabwe and South Africa later in 2005, where he played in six ODIs. His performances were modest, and he scored 167 runs at an average of 27.83. He was dropped from the one-day side at the start of the 2005–06 season in favour of more aggressive openers, and the national selectors indicated that they regarded him primarily as a long-form player.

= = = Test career = = =

Rennick made his Test debut against the West Indies at Seddon Park in Hamilton in March 2006. Opening the batting, he scored 9 in the first innings and 46 in the second, sharing a stand of 93 for the first wicket. He kept his place for the second Test at the Basin Reserve, where he scored 27 and 18.

After his record-breaking 2006–07 domestic season, he was recalled for the home series against Bangladesh in 2008. In the second Test at the University Oval, his home ground, he made his only Test century, scoring 117 in the first innings. He batted for more than six hours, and the innings was greeted by a standing ovation from a crowd that included many spectators from Southland and Otago. *The Otago Daily Times* described it as "an innings built in the manner of the man: carefully, without fuss, and with very few mistakes".

He toured England in 2008, playing in two of the three Test matches. Facing the swinging ball in early-season conditions, he struggled and made 63 runs in four innings, with a top score of 29 at Old Trafford. He was dropped for the final Test of the series.

His final Test appearances came during a home series against Pakistan in late 2009 and early 2010, in which he played two matches as a replacement for an injured opener. He scored 41 and 12 in the first of these, and 3 and 22 in the second, and was not selected again. In nine Tests he scored 412 runs at an average of 25.75, with one century and two half-centuries.

= = = Later ODI appearances = = =

Rennick returned to the one-day side briefly in 2009 for a tri-series in Australia, where his calm batting was intended to provide stability to a top order that had been prone to collapses. He scored 63 against Australia at the Adelaide Oval, his highest ODI score, but made only 38 runs in his other three innings. He played his last ODI against India at Westpac Stadium in Wellington in 2010. He finished his ODI career with 541 runs at 26.05 and took two wickets with his occasional medium pace.

= = County cricket = =

Rennick played two seasons of English county cricket as an overseas player. In 2008, after the New Zealand tour of England, he joined Leicestershire for the second half of the season, replacing an overseas player who had been called up for international duty. He scored 489 runs in seven County Championship matches, including a century against Glamorgan at Grace Road.

In 2012 he signed for Worcestershire as a Kolpak-ineligible overseas professional for the first two months of the English season. He scored 612 runs in eight Championship matches at an average of 47.07, including two centuries, and was praised by the club for his work with younger batsmen. He declined an offer to return in 2013, citing family commitments.

He also played several seasons of club cricket in England early in his career, including spells with clubs in the Lancashire League and the Northern Premier League between 2001 and 2004, which he credited with improving his technique against seam bowling.

= = Playing style = =

Rennick was a left-handed batsman who played predominantly off the back foot. He was regarded as having a sound defensive technique, and commentators frequently referred to his discipline in leaving balls outside the off stump. His main scoring shots were the cut, the nudge off the hips, and the cover drive against fuller deliveries. He was not considered a powerful hitter, and his limited-overs strike rate was below that of most of his contemporaries.

Former New Zealand coach Peter Harland described him as "the sort of player who would bat all day if you let him, and who made bowlers earn every wicket". Critics noted that he could become stuck at the crease against accurate spin bowling, and that his slow scoring sometimes placed pressure on his partners. His difficulties in English conditions were attributed to his tendency to play with his bat away from his body when driving.

As a bowler, Rennick bowled gentle right-arm medium pace, mainly used to break partnerships or to allow frontline bowlers a rest. He took 23 first-class wickets in his career, with best figures of 3 for 31 against Wellington in 2005. He was a reliable fielder, usually stationed in the slips or at gully, and took 104 first-class catches.

= = Coaching and administrative career = =

After retiring from playing, Rennick returned to Dunedin and was appointed batting coach of Otago in 2014. He held the position for four seasons, during which several Otago batsmen progressed to national selection. In 2018 he was appointed head coach of the Otago Volts, and he led the side to victory in the domestic one-day competition in the 2019–20 season, Otago's first one-day title in more than a decade.

He stepped down as head coach in 2022 and was subsequently appointed to the New Zealand national selection panel, with particular responsibility for the domestic first-class game and the development of players in the southern provinces. He has also worked as a cricket commentator for regional radio and as a summariser on domestic broadcasts.

Rennick has been a vocal advocate for the preservation of first-class cricket in New Zealand, and has spoken publicly about the challenges facing the Plunket Shield in an era dominated by franchise Twenty20 leagues. In a 2021 interview he said that the four-day game "is still the place where cricketers learn who they really are".

= = Personal life = =

Rennick married Sarah Donnelly, a physiotherapist he met while studying at the University of Otago, in 2006. The couple have two children. His decision to move to Canterbury in 2011 was partly prompted by his wife's work at a Christchurch hospital, and the family returned to Dunedin in 2014.

His younger sister, Emma Rennick, played netball for Otago at provincial level. Rennick is a supporter of the Highlanders rugby team and has been involved in fundraising for rural sports clubs in Southland. In 2016 he helped establish a coaching programme for children in small Southland communities, which provides equipment and visiting coaches to schools without established cricket teams.

In 2020 he published an autobiography, *The Long Game*, written with the sports journalist Hamish Clark. The book covered his upbringing in rural Southland, his international career and his views on the development of the game in New Zealand. It was shortlisted for a New Zealand sports book award.

= = Honours and awards = =

Rennick was named Otago's player of the year on three occasions, in 2003–04, 2006–07 and 2009–10. He was the domestic player of the season at the New Zealand Cricket awards in 2007. His 241 against Northern Districts remained the highest individual first-class score by an Otago batsman at the University Oval for several years. In 2015 he was made a life member of the Otago Cricket Association in recognition of his services to cricket in the province.

= = Career statistics = =

In 118 first-class matches between 2001 and 2014, Rennick scored 7,412 runs at an average of 38.21, with 19 centuries and 37 half-centuries. His highest score was 241. He took 23 wickets at an average of 41.30 and held 104 catches.

In 97 List A matches he scored 2,866 runs at an average of 32.94, with three centuries and a highest score of 121 not out. In Twenty20 cricket he played 28 matches, scoring 498 runs.

At international level he played nine Test matches, scoring 412 runs at an average of 25.75 with a highest score of 117, and 22 One Day Internationals, scoring 541 runs at an average of 26.05 with a highest score of 63.
