**2033 Formula One World Championship**

The **2033 Formula One World Championship** was the 84th season of the FIA Formula One World Championship, a motor racing championship for single-seater cars recognised by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). The championship was contested over twenty-three Grands Prix, beginning in Melbourne on 13 March and ending in Abu Dhabi on 4 December. Drivers and teams competed for the World Drivers' Championship and the World Constructors' Championship.

Tobias Lindqvist, driving for Halvorsen Racing, won his first World Drivers' Championship at the season-ending Abu Dhabi Grand Prix. He finished four points ahead of Matteo Ferracini of Scuderia Valtorre. The title margin was the smallest since the introduction of the current points system. Valtorre won the World Constructors' Championship, its first since 2019, on the strength of consistent results from both of its drivers.

The season was the second under the technical regulations introduced in 2032. It was notable for the debut of the Kestrel Motorsport team, the return of the Turkish Grand Prix to the calendar, and a mid-season dispute over the legality of front-wing designs that led to a clarification from the FIA in July.

= = Background = =

The 2032 season had been dominated by Arden Grand Prix. Arden's lead driver, Callum Reyes, won eleven races on his way to a second consecutive title. Arden entered 2033 as favourites, but several rival teams had made large investments over the winter. Halvorsen Racing, based in Brackley, had hired a new technical director, and Valtorre had opened an upgraded wind tunnel at its Maranello-area facility in late 2032.

The 2032 regulations had reduced the size of the cars, introduced fully sustainable fuels and raised the share of power drawn from the hybrid system to roughly half of total output. Most teams had struggled with energy deployment during the first year of the rules. Pre-season observers expected the field to converge in performance as teams better understood the regulations.

= = Entries = =

Eleven teams and twenty-two drivers were entered in the championship. All teams used tyres supplied by Pirelli. Four power unit manufacturers supplied engines to the field.

Kestrel Motorsport, an American-owned team based in Silverstone, became the eleventh entrant after its application was approved by the FIA in 2031 and by the commercial rights holder in early 2032. Kestrel used customer power units supplied by Valtorre.

= = = Team changes = = =

Kestrel Motorsport made its debut, increasing the grid to twenty-two cars for the first time since 2016. The team's base had previously belonged to a defunct sports car outfit and was refurbished in 2031 and 2032.

Ridgeway F1 changed power unit supplier, moving from Arden to Aurelle on a five-year agreement. Strada Racing, formerly known as Strada Lorenzi, adopted a shortened name following a change in ownership.

= = = Driver changes = = =

Tobias Lindqvist moved from Marlow Racing to Halvorsen Racing, replacing the retired Georg Steinmann, who had driven for the team since 2025. Oliver Pemberton was promoted from Halvorsen's junior programme to take Lindqvist's seat at Marlow.

Sofia Lindgren joined Aurelle after two seasons in Formula 2, in which she finished third and then first. She became the first woman to start a World Championship race since 1976 when she took part in the Australian Grand Prix.

Kestrel signed Ryan Castellano, an American driver who had raced for Sentinel in 2031 and 2032, and Thomas Vermeulen, who had spent the previous year as a reserve driver. Emre Kaya made his debut for Sentinel after finishing runner-up in Formula 2.

= = = Mid-season changes = = =

Joaquín Salas missed the Belgian and Dutch Grands Prix after fracturing his wrist in a cycling accident during the summer break. He was replaced by Brenner reserve driver Felix Amsel. Salas returned at the Italian Grand Prix.

Noah Whitfield was released by Strada Racing after the Singapore Grand Prix. The team cited "a difference in expectations". He was replaced for the remainder of the season by Formula 2 driver Andrés Pacheco.

= = Calendar = =

The championship consisted of twenty-three rounds. The calendar was published by the FIA World Motor Sport Council in September 2032.

The Turkish Grand Prix returned to the calendar at Istanbul Park after an absence of several years, replacing the Chinese Grand Prix, which was omitted due to construction at the Shanghai International Circuit. The Monaco Grand Prix was held under a revised three-year contract that moved it to the first weekend of June.

Six rounds used the sprint format, in which a shorter race was held on Saturday. The sprint rounds were held in Bahrain, Miami, Austria, Belgium, Austin and Brazil.

= = Regulation changes = =

= = = Technical regulations = = =

The 2033 technical regulations were largely unchanged from 2032. The FIA introduced minor revisions to improve the performance of cars running closely behind one another. These included a modified floor edge profile and a simplified rear-wing endplate.

The minimum weight of the cars was reduced by 6 kilograms, to 762 kilograms. The reduction followed requests from several teams who had found the 2032 target difficult to meet.

Following the Canadian Grand Prix, the FIA issued a technical directive clarifying the permitted deflection of front-wing elements under load. The directive took effect from the British Grand Prix. It followed complaints from several teams that rival front wings flexed at high speed, reducing drag on straights. Arden and Aurelle were widely reported to be most affected, though neither team was found to have breached the regulations.

= = = Sporting regulations = = =

The cost cap was raised from $140 million to $146 million to account for inflation and the addition of a twenty-third race. New teams were permitted an additional allowance during their first two seasons, which Kestrel used to build its operational infrastructure.

The sprint format was revised so that the sprint race and its qualifying session were held on Saturday, with Grand Prix qualifying moved back to Friday afternoon at sprint weekends. The change was intended to make Saturday a self-contained event.

A new rule required every team to run a driver with no more than two previous Grand Prix starts in at least two Friday practice sessions per car during the season, an increase from one session per car in previous years.

= = Season summary = =

= = = Opening rounds = = =

The season began at the Australian Grand Prix, where Callum Reyes took pole position for Arden. Reyes led the opening stint but lost the lead to Matteo Ferracini after a slow pit stop. Ferracini won the race, his first since 2030, ahead of Reyes and Tobias Lindqvist. Sofia Lindgren finished thirteenth on her debut.

Arden responded in Bahrain, where Reyes won both the sprint and the Grand Prix. Lindqvist finished second in the main race after holding off Ferracini during a late safety car restart. In Saudi Arabia, Lindqvist took his first pole position for Halvorsen and converted it into victory, the first win of his career. A collision on the opening lap between Daniel Okafor and Lucas Thibault brought out the safety car and eliminated both drivers.

At Suzuka, rain during qualifying led to a mixed grid, with Kenji Arakawa qualifying fourth for Marlow in front of his home crowd. The race was held in dry conditions and was won by Reyes. Arakawa finished sixth, which was then his best result. After four rounds, Reyes led the championship by nine points from Lindqvist.

= = = European season and front-wing dispute = = =

Lindqvist won the Miami sprint, and Ferracini won the Grand Prix after Reyes received a five-second penalty for leaving the track and gaining an advantage. At Imola, Halvorsen introduced a substantial upgrade package, and Lindqvist won from pole. Rafael Domínguez finished second to give Halvorsen its first one-two finish since 2027.

The Monaco Grand Prix was disrupted by a heavy shower in the middle of the race. Étienne Marchal of Aurelle switched to intermediate tyres one lap before his rivals and won the race, Aurelle's first victory in five seasons. Ferracini finished second and Lindqvist third. Reyes crashed at the swimming pool section during the rain and retired.

Reyes returned to winning form in Spain and Canada. In Montreal he started from pole and led every lap, while Lindqvist retired with a hydraulic failure. After the race, Valtorre and Halvorsen jointly asked the FIA to examine the front-wing flexibility of several cars. The FIA issued its technical directive shortly afterwards. Arden team principal Simon Hartley described the directive as "a clarification, not a change", but acknowledged that the team would need to modify its wing design.

= = = Mid-season = = =

At the Austrian Grand Prix, Valtorre won both races, with Thibault taking the sprint and Ferracini the Grand Prix. The British Grand Prix, the first race held under the revised front-wing directive, saw Arden fall behind its rivals. Reyes qualified seventh and finished fifth. Lindqvist won in front of a large crowd, having lived in the United Kingdom since his junior career. Oliver Pemberton finished third for Marlow, recording the first podium of his career.

At the Hungaroring, Ferracini won from pole ahead of Lindqvist. Entering the summer break, Ferracini led the championship with 241 points, followed by Lindqvist on 236 and Reyes on 219.

= = = Post-summer break = = =

The Belgian round was affected by heavy rain on Saturday, and the sprint race was cancelled after two laps behind the safety car. Lindqvist won the Grand Prix on Sunday in dry conditions. At Zandvoort, Okafor took his first career win after Reyes and Lindqvist collided at the Tarzan corner on lap 37. The stewards found Reyes predominantly to blame and gave him a ten-second penalty and two penalty points on his licence.

At Monza, Thibault won his first race of the season, and the result helped Valtorre move ahead of Arden in the Constructors' Championship. The Turkish Grand Prix, held for the first time in several years, was won by Ferracini. Henrik Aalto finished fourth for Team Corvane, the team's best result of the season.

In Singapore, a late safety car caused by Noah Whitfield's crash allowed Reyes to pit for fresh tyres and overtake Lindqvist for the win with three laps remaining. Whitfield was released by Strada the following week.

= = = Concluding rounds = = =

The Americas rounds produced three different winners. Lindqvist won the sprint and the Grand Prix in Austin. In Mexico City, Ferracini won as Lindqvist struggled with brake temperatures and finished sixth. In São Paulo, Pemberton won a rain-affected sprint, while Reyes won the Grand Prix. Reyes was mathematically eliminated from title contention at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, which was won by Ferracini after Lindqvist was delayed by a slow pit stop.

Lindqvist won in Qatar, where Ferracini finished third behind Domínguez. As a result, Lindqvist entered the final round with a two-point lead.

At the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, Ferracini qualified on pole and Lindqvist second. Ferracini led for the first 28 laps before Halvorsen made an early pit stop with Lindqvist, who rejoined in clear air and gained the lead when Ferracini made his own stop four laps later. Lindqvist held the lead to the finish, while Ferracini finished second. Lindqvist became the first Swedish World Champion. Valtorre's results secured the Constructors' Championship by 31 points over Halvorsen.

= = Reception and aftermath = =

The 2033 season was generally well received by commentators, who noted that six different drivers won Grands Prix and that the championship was not decided until the final race. Television audiences rose in several markets compared with 2032, particularly in the United States and Scandinavia.

The front-wing directive was widely discussed during the season. Some analysts described it as the turning point of the championship, citing Arden's loss of form in the British and Hungarian rounds. Arden disputed this view, noting that Reyes won three further races after the directive came into force.

Kestrel Motorsport scored its first points at the Singapore Grand Prix, when Ryan Castellano finished eighth. The team finished last in the Constructors' Championship but was described by its owners as having met its targets for its first season.

Lindqvist's title made him the twelfth driver to win the championship in his first season with a new team. Ferracini said after the final race that the season had been "the best of my career, even without the title". Both drivers remained with their teams for the 2034 season.
