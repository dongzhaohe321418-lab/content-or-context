The **1974 Western Plains Football League season** was the 52nd season of the Western Plains Football League (WPFL), an Australian rules football competition in the central-west region of New South Wales. The season ran from 13 April to 14 September 1974 and was contested by ten clubs. Bellhurst won its fourth premiership, defeating Carrabool by 19 points in the grand final at Memorial Oval, Dunmore. The season was the first in which the league used a final five system, and it was the last before the league's expansion to twelve clubs in 1975.

= = Background = =

The Western Plains Football League was formed in 1922 as the Dunmore and District Football Association. It took its present name in 1947, when it merged with the smaller Upper Lachlan Football Union. During the 1950s and 1960s the league fielded between eight and eleven senior clubs, most of them drawn from wheat and wool towns within roughly 150 kilometres of Dunmore. Australian rules football was a minority code in much of the region, where rugby league was dominant. The WPFL's survival was often credited to the presence of large numbers of Victorian and South Australian settlers in the district during the soldier settlement schemes after both world wars.

Before the 1974 season the league's annual general meeting, held at the Dunmore Railway Institute on 9 February, approved several changes to the competition. The most significant was the replacement of the final four, which the league had used since 1931, with a final five modelled on the system adopted by the Victorian Football League in 1972. Delegates also approved a request from the Tarrin Downs Football Club to rejoin the league after a two-year recess, bringing the number of clubs to ten. A proposal by Kellaway to introduce a reserves competition for players over 30 was defeated by six votes to four.

The 1973 premiers were Carrabool, which had defeated Mount Ossory by 31 points in the previous year's grand final. Carrabool entered the 1974 season as favourites, having retained most of its senior list and recruited former South Melbourne reserves player Graham Tolhurst as playing coach.

= = Clubs = =

Ten clubs took part in the 1974 season.

* **Bellhurst** (the Magpies) – based at Bellhurst Recreation Reserve. Coached by Neville Prentice.
* **Carrabool** (the Bulldogs) – based at Carrabool Showground. Coached by Graham Tolhurst.
* **Dunmore** (the Eagles) – based at Memorial Oval, Dunmore. Coached by Ray Fennessy.
* **Dunmore Rovers** (the Rovers) – based at Halloran Park, Dunmore. Coached by Kevin Mulqueen.
* **Gilgarra** (the Swans) – based at Gilgarra Oval. Coached by Barry Hosking.
* **Kellaway** (the Tigers) – based at Kellaway Park. Coached by Des Larkin.
* **Mount Ossory** (the Blues) – based at Ossory Reserve. Coached by Peter Vandenberg.
* **Narrangullen** (the Hawks) – based at Narrangullen Sportsground. Coached by Trevor McAuliffe.
* **Tarrin Downs** (the Demons) – based at Tarrin Downs Oval. Coached by Alan "Snowy" Richter.
* **Wyalla** (the Saints) – based at Wyalla Park. Coached by John Corbett.

= = Pre-season = =

The pre-season was marked by an unusually large number of player movements between clubs. Under the league's permit system, which allowed players to transfer between clubs with the consent of both clubs and the league's permit committee, 41 clearances were granted between November 1973 and March 1974, a record at the time. The largest number of new players went to Tarrin Downs, which, having been in recess, had only eleven players from its 1971 list available. The club recruited heavily from the neighbouring Lachlan Valley Football League and from the Canberra district, and was granted special dispensation to field up to four players who had not lived in the district for the required six months.

Bellhurst appointed Neville Prentice, a former Wyalla ruckman, as coach in October 1973, replacing Leo Dwyer, who had retired after six seasons. Prentice had not coached at senior level before. Bellhurst had finished fifth in 1973 and was not widely regarded as a contender.

The annual pre-season lightning premiership was held at Gilgarra Oval on 30 March. It was won by Mount Ossory, which defeated Dunmore in the final.

= = Home-and-away season = =

The home-and-away season consisted of 18 rounds, with each club playing the others twice. Matches were played on Saturday afternoons, with the exception of the Anzac Day round, which was played on Thursday, 25 April.

= = = Rounds 1–6 = = =

Carrabool began the season strongly, winning its first five matches by an average margin of 42 points. Its round 3 defeat of Narrangullen, by 87 points, was the largest winning margin of the season. Tolhurst kicked seven goals in that match and was reported by the Dunmore Advocate as being "comfortably the best player seen in the league in a decade".

Tarrin Downs, despite its recruiting, lost its first four matches. Its first win since returning to the league came in round 5, when it defeated Kellaway by three points at Tarrin Downs Oval. The match was played in heavy rain and the final score was 6.14 (50) to 7.5 (47).

Bellhurst lost two of its first three matches, including a 44-point defeat by Dunmore in round 2, but then won three consecutive games. In round 6 it ended Carrabool's unbeaten run with a nine-point win at Bellhurst Recreation Reserve, in front of a crowd estimated at 2,100, the largest at the ground since 1964. Bellhurst full-forward Colin Mapleson kicked five goals.

= = = Rounds 7–12 = = =

The middle part of the season was disrupted by weather. A prolonged wet period through June caused the round 9 match between Gilgarra and Wyalla to be postponed when the Wyalla ground was declared unfit; it was eventually played on 3 August, in the league's scheduled bye week. Several other matches were played on heavily waterlogged grounds, and scoring in the competition fell markedly. Across rounds 8 to 11 the average combined score per match was 121 points, compared with 168 points in the first seven rounds.

Mount Ossory and Dunmore emerged as Carrabool's main rivals during this period. Mount Ossory won six consecutive matches between rounds 6 and 11, a sequence ended by Carrabool in round 12. Dunmore, led by its captain and centreman Michael Treloar, lost only once between rounds 5 and 12.

In round 10 the match between Dunmore Rovers and Kellaway was marred by a brawl late in the third quarter involving most players from both sides. Four players were reported by the field umpire, and the league tribunal later suspended Kellaway's Steven Gorringe for eight matches for striking, the longest suspension imposed in the league since 1958. Dunmore Rovers was fined $100 and Kellaway $150 for failing to control their players and officials.

= = = Rounds 13–18 = = =

The race for the final five tightened in the second half of the season. By the end of round 14, only three wins separated second place from seventh. Bellhurst won five of its last six matches, including a 15-point defeat of Mount Ossory in round 16 that effectively secured its place in the finals.

Gilgarra and Narrangullen contested fifth position until the final round. Gilgarra, needing to win against Tarrin Downs and for Narrangullen to lose to Dunmore, achieved both results. Narrangullen lost by 22 points to Dunmore, and Gilgarra defeated Tarrin Downs by 40 points, finishing level with Narrangullen on wins but ahead on percentage.

Carrabool finished on top of the ladder for the second consecutive season, losing only three matches. Tarrin Downs finished last, with three wins.

= = Ladder = =

The final home-and-away ladder was as follows. Four premiership points were awarded for a win and two for a draw.

1. Carrabool – 15 wins, 3 losses, 60 points (percentage 148.2)
2. Dunmore – 13 wins, 5 losses, 52 points (131.7)
3. Mount Ossory – 12 wins, 6 losses, 48 points (122.4)
4. Bellhurst – 12 wins, 6 losses, 48 points (115.9)
5. Gilgarra – 9 wins, 9 losses, 36 points (101.3)
6. Narrangullen – 9 wins, 9 losses, 36 points (97.6)
7. Wyalla – 8 wins, 10 losses, 32 points (94.0)
8. Dunmore Rovers – 6 wins, 11 losses, 1 draw, 26 points (83.5)
9. Kellaway – 4 wins, 13 losses, 1 draw, 18 points (72.1)
10. Tarrin Downs – 3 wins, 15 losses, 12 points (61.8)

The only drawn match of the season was between Dunmore Rovers and Kellaway in round 15, which finished 9.11 (65) apiece.

= = Finals series = =

Under the final five system adopted for 1974, the second- and third-placed clubs met in the qualifying final, while the fourth- and fifth-placed clubs met in the elimination final. The minor premier received a bye in the first week. All finals were played at Memorial Oval, Dunmore.

= = = Week one = = =

In the qualifying final on 17 August, Dunmore defeated Mount Ossory by 12 points, 13.9 (87) to 11.9 (75). Treloar was named best on ground.

In the elimination final on the same day, Bellhurst defeated Gilgarra by 51 points, 17.15 (117) to 9.12 (66). Mapleson kicked six goals. Gilgarra's season ended, and the club announced the retirement of coach Barry Hosking after the match.

= = = Week two = = =

In the second semi-final on 24 August, Carrabool defeated Dunmore by 26 points, 14.12 (96) to 10.10 (70), advancing directly to the grand final.

In the first semi-final, Bellhurst defeated Mount Ossory by four points, 10.8 (68) to 9.10 (64), in a match played in strong crosswinds. Mount Ossory had a shot at goal after the final siren from 35 metres, but the kick by full-forward Daryl Kiernan fell short.

= = = Preliminary final = = =

The preliminary final on 31 August between Dunmore and Bellhurst was regarded by contemporary reports as the best match of the season. Dunmore led by 23 points at three-quarter time, but Bellhurst kicked six goals to one in the final quarter to win by seven points, 14.13 (97) to 13.12 (90). Bellhurst rover Terry Cusack kicked the match-winning goal with less than two minutes remaining.

= = Grand final = =

The 1974 grand final was played on Saturday, 14 September, at Memorial Oval, Dunmore, in front of an attendance of 6,412, then a league record. The match was delayed by two weeks from its originally scheduled date because of a dispute between the league and the Dunmore Municipal Council over ground hire charges, which was resolved on 4 September.

Carrabool started as favourite and led by 11 points at quarter time. Bellhurst gained control of the ruck contests in the second quarter, with Prentice moving himself into the ruck, and led by three points at half time. The third quarter was even, and Bellhurst led by eight points at the final change. In the last quarter Bellhurst kicked four goals to two, with Mapleson kicking two of them.

The final score was Bellhurst 15.11 (101) defeated Carrabool 12.10 (82).

Mapleson finished with five goals and Cusack with three. Tolhurst kicked four goals for Carrabool. Bellhurst half-back flanker Ian Vosper was awarded the Harold Gee Medal as the best player on the ground; it was the first time the medal, introduced in 1968, had been awarded to a defender.

The premiership was Bellhurst's first since 1959 and its fourth overall, following wins in 1933, 1951 and 1959. Prentice became the first coach in the league's history to win a premiership in his first season at senior level.

= = Awards = =

= = = Lachlan Medal = = =

The Lachlan Medal, awarded to the fairest and best player in the league as judged by field umpires on a 3–2–1 basis in each home-and-away match, was won by Michael Treloar of Dunmore with 24 votes. Treloar had finished second in 1972 and third in 1973. Graham Tolhurst of Carrabool, who polled 22 votes, finished second, and Ian Vosper of Bellhurst finished third with 19 votes.

Two players who would otherwise have finished in the top ten were ineligible because of suspensions received during the season.

= = = Leading goalkicker = = =

The Coleman–Price Trophy for the leading goalkicker in the home-and-away season was won by Colin Mapleson of Bellhurst with 74 goals. Including finals, Mapleson kicked 92 goals for the season, the highest season total in the league since 1966. Tolhurst finished second with 68 goals, and Daryl Kiernan of Mount Ossory third with 57.

* **Umpire of the year** – Leonard Coote

= = Reserves and juniors = =

The reserves premiership was won by Dunmore, which defeated Carrabool by 33 points in the reserves grand final played as a curtain-raiser to the senior match. It was Dunmore's third consecutive reserves premiership.

The under-17 competition, administered jointly with the Dunmore Junior Football Association, was won by Mount Ossory. A record 23 teams competed across the league's three junior age groups in 1974, reflecting a growth in junior participation that the league attributed to a schools program begun in 1972.

= = Representative football = =

The WPFL competed in the New South Wales Country Football Championships, held in Wagga Wagga over the June long weekend. The league's representative side, captained by Treloar and coached by Ray Fennessy, defeated the Lachlan Valley Football League and the Far West Football League before losing the final to the Riverina representative side by 28 points. Tolhurst and Vosper were selected in the championship team of the carnival.

= = Off-field matters = =

The league recorded an operating surplus of $2,870 for the 1974 season, its largest to that time, owing largely to finals and grand final gate receipts. The financial position of individual clubs varied considerably. Tarrin Downs reported a loss and required a $600 loan from the league to complete the season.

Television coverage of the league began during the season, when the regional station CWN-6 broadcast delayed highlights of selected matches on Sunday evenings. The grand final was the first WPFL match to be filmed in its entirety. Radio station 2DM continued its live broadcast of one match each week, a practice that had begun in 1956.

In July the league received an application from the Ferndale Football Club, from the Ferndale Lakes district east of Dunmore, to join the competition. A second application, from a proposed combined Yarrowie–Bendemeer club, was received in August. Both applications were accepted at a special general meeting held on 19 October 1974, expanding the league to twelve clubs for the 1975 season.

= = Aftermath = =

Bellhurst was unable to defend its premiership in 1975, losing the first semi-final to Dunmore. Prentice coached the club until the end of the 1977 season and later served as league president from 1984 to 1989.

Carrabool reached the grand final again in 1975 and won the premiership, defeating Mount Ossory. Tolhurst coached the club for a further three seasons before returning to Victoria.

Mapleson's 92 goals remained the club record at Bellhurst until 1988. Treloar won a second Lachlan Medal in 1976 and was among the inaugural inductees to the Western Plains Football League Hall of Fame when it was established in 1997. The final five system introduced in 1974 was retained by the league until 1983, when it was replaced by a final six to accommodate the expanded competition.

The 1974 grand final was commemorated by Bellhurst in 2004 with a 30-year reunion of the premiership team, at which a plaque recording the names of the players was unveiled at the Bellhurst Recreation Reserve grandstand.
