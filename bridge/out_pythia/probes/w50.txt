 = Gravewright discography = 

The discography of Gravewright, a Finnish heavy metal band formed in Tampere in 1991, consists of nine studio albums, two live albums, three compilation albums, four extended plays, one box set, and twenty-two singles. The band has also released four video albums and appeared on a number of tribute and various-artist compilations. Gravewright's recorded output spans more than three decades and reflects a gradual shift from the raw doom metal of its earliest demos toward a more melodic, progressive style that incorporates elements of Finnish folk music and symphonic arrangements.

Gravewright signed its first recording contract with the Helsinki-based independent label Kalmo Records in 1994, and remained with the label until 2001, when it moved to the German label Nachtwerk Musik. Since 2015 the band has released its recordings through its own imprint, Rautaportti, with international distribution handled by a series of partner labels. The band's most commercially successful album, Lumen Ferreum (2004), reached number two on the Official Finnish Charts and was certified platinum by Musiikkituottajat, the Finnish music industry association. In total, the band has placed seven albums in the Finnish top ten.

 = = History = = 

 = = = Early recordings (1991–1996) = = = 

Gravewright was founded in the autumn of 1991 by guitarist Jarno Väisälä and bassist Tuomas Rekola, who had previously played together in the short-lived thrash metal group Kylmä Pato. The pair recruited vocalist Eero Lindqvist and drummer Mikko Haapanen within several months, and the four-piece recorded its first demo, Tuhkan Alla ("Beneath the Ashes"), in a rented rehearsal space in the Hervanta district of Tampere in March 1992. The demo was distributed on cassette in an edition of approximately 300 copies, most of which were sold at local concerts or traded through the underground tape-trading network then active in northern Europe.

A second demo, Seven Wounds of the North, followed in 1993 and attracted the attention of Kalmo Records, a small label specialising in doom and death metal. Kalmo signed the band in early 1994 and issued its debut album, Marrow of Winter, in November of that year. The album was recorded over eleven days at Studio Pohjola in Jyväskylä with producer Ari Kesti, and was noted for its slow tempos, down-tuned guitars, and lengthy song structures; its closing track, "The Long Thaw", runs for just under fourteen minutes. Marrow of Winter did not chart, but it received favourable notices in several European fanzines and in the Finnish music magazine Rumba.

The band's second album, Hollow Crown, was released in 1996. It was the first Gravewright record to feature keyboardist Liisa Orava, who joined in 1995 and whose use of Hammond organ and Mellotron sounds became a defining feature of the band's middle period. Hollow Crown entered the Finnish album chart at number 31, the band's first chart appearance.

 = = = Breakthrough and mainstream success (1997–2004) = = = 

Gravewright's third album, Ashen Psalter (1998), marked a turning point in the band's commercial fortunes. The single "Kivinen Rukous" ("Stone Prayer"), one of only two songs on the album sung in Finnish, received regular airplay on the national radio station YleX and reached number nine on the Finnish singles chart. The album peaked at number six. Drummer Mikko Haapanen left the band shortly after the album's release, citing exhaustion from touring, and was replaced by Sami Kaukonen, who has remained a member since.

In 1999 the band issued its first EP, Bridges Burned, which collected four outtakes from the Ashen Psalter sessions alongside a cover of "Wasteland Hymn" by the Swedish band Ödesmark. The following year Kalmo Records released Seven Wounds: The Demos 1992–1993, a compilation of the band's early demo recordings remastered from the original tapes.

Gravewright's fourth album, The Cartographer's Lament (2001), was the band's last for Kalmo Records and its first to be released outside the Nordic countries, through a licensing agreement with Nachtwerk Musik. The album was a loose concept record following an eighteenth-century surveyor mapping the Finnish–Russian border, and featured guest appearances by members of the Tampere Philharmonic Orchestra. It reached number four in Finland and number 58 on the German Media Control charts.

Following the expiry of the Kalmo contract, the band signed directly with Nachtwerk Musik in late 2001. Its first release for the label, the live album Iron Throats: Live in Oulu (2002), was recorded over two nights at the Teatria venue in Oulu during the tour supporting The Cartographer's Lament.

The band's fifth studio album, Lumen Ferreum, was released in March 2004. Produced by Swedish engineer Henrik Ståhlberg at Atlantis Studio in Gothenburg, the album featured a cleaner and more polished sound than its predecessors. Its lead single, "Lantern Bearer", became the band's highest-charting single, reaching number two in Finland, and the follow-up, "A Northern Mass", reached number five. Lumen Ferreum peaked at number two on the Finnish album chart, where it remained in the top forty for thirty-one weeks, and was certified platinum for shipments of 30,000 copies. It also charted in Germany, Austria, Sweden and Switzerland. The album is generally regarded by critics as the band's most accomplished work, and in 2014 it was ranked at number 17 in Inferno magazine's list of the greatest Finnish metal albums.

 = = = Lineup changes and experimentation (2005–2013) = = = 

Founding vocalist Eero Lindqvist departed Gravewright in 2005 to concentrate on his solo career. He was replaced after an extended search by Antti Railo, previously of the progressive metal group Aavikko Sun. Railo's broader vocal range and background in choral singing influenced the direction of the band's sixth album, Saltwater Liturgy (2007), which included extended passages of layered clean vocals and a twelve-minute title suite. The album reached number three in Finland and was certified gold, though some reviewers and long-time listeners criticised what they regarded as a move away from the band's doom metal roots.

In 2008 Nachtwerk released the compilation Lanterns and Ashes: 1994–2007, which included tracks from each of the band's first six albums as well as two newly recorded songs, "Glass Furnace" and "Ember Dialect". The compilation reached number eight in Finland.

The band's seventh album, Of Rust and Reverie (2010), was its most experimental release. It featured kantele, jouhikko and other traditional Finnish instruments performed by guest musicians from the Sibelius Academy folk music department, as well as an extended collaboration with the Finnish contemporary composer Helena Vartiainen on the closing track, "Rautakausi" ("Iron Age"). The album reached number five in Finland. Keyboardist Liisa Orava left the band following the supporting tour in 2011 and was replaced by Ville Sorsa.

A second live album, Vigil at Tavastia, was recorded at Tavastia Club in Helsinki in December 2011 and released in 2012 as both a double album and a video album. The same year the band released the EP Four Winters, a set of acoustic reinterpretations of earlier songs recorded at a lakeside cabin in Ruovesi.

 = = = Independent era (2014–present) = = = 

Gravewright's contract with Nachtwerk Musik ended in 2014, and in early 2015 the band announced the formation of its own label, Rautaportti ("Iron Gate"). The band's first independent release, the EP Gate of Iron (2015), was funded in part by advance orders from the band's fan club and was distributed internationally by the Dutch company Blackmere Distribution.

The eighth studio album, The Weight of Lanterns, was released in September 2016. Produced by guitarist Jarno Väisälä and mixed by Henrik Ståhlberg, it was widely described as a return to the heavier style of the band's early recordings while retaining the melodic and folk elements of its later work. The album debuted at number one on the Finnish album chart, the band's first and to date only chart-topping release, and was nominated for the Emma Award for Metal Album of the Year.

In 2019, to mark the twenty-fifth anniversary of Marrow of Winter, Rautaportti issued the box set Iron and Ash: 1991–2016, comprising remastered editions of all eight studio albums, both live albums, the original demos, and a previously unreleased album-length recording of a 1997 rehearsal session. The set was limited to 2,500 numbered copies and sold out within six weeks of release.

The band's ninth studio album, Northbound Requiem, was released in April 2023 after a delay attributed in part to the cancellation of planned recording sessions during 2020 and 2021. The album reached number two in Finland and was the band's first record to chart in the United Kingdom, reaching number 87 on the UK Rock & Metal Albums Chart. Bassist Tuomas Rekola announced his retirement from touring in 2024, though he remains a member of the band for recording purposes; live bass duties have since been performed by Oskari Lehtimäki.

 = = Live albums = = 

Iron Throats: Live in Oulu (2002) was recorded on 11 and 12 October 2001 and released on Nachtwerk Musik in June 2002. It contains fifteen songs drawn from the band's first four albums and peaked at number 14 in Finland.

Vigil at Tavastia (2012) was recorded on 17 December 2011 and released in October 2012 by Nachtwerk Musik as a two-disc CD, a triple LP, and a DVD and Blu-ray video album. It reached number 9 in Finland and was the band's final release for the label.

 = = Compilation albums = = 

Seven Wounds: The Demos 1992–1993 (2000), released by Kalmo Records, collects the complete contents of the band's two demo cassettes. A vinyl edition was issued by Rautaportti in 2017.

Lanterns and Ashes: 1994–2007 (2008), released by Nachtwerk Musik, is a career retrospective containing eighteen tracks, including two previously unreleased songs. It reached number 8 in Finland.

B-Sides from the Iron Age (2018), released by Rautaportti, compiles non-album tracks, single B-sides and cover versions recorded between 1998 and 2016. It did not chart but was released on limited-edition coloured vinyl for Record Store Day.

 = = Extended plays = = 

Bridges Burned (1999), Kalmo Records. Contains four outtakes from Ashen Psalter and a cover of "Wasteland Hymn".

Under a Copper Moon (2005), Nachtwerk Musik. The final release to feature vocalist Eero Lindqvist, issued as a farewell recording; it reached number 12 in Finland.

Four Winters (2012), Nachtwerk Musik. Acoustic reinterpretations of "The Long Thaw", "Kivinen Rukous", "Lantern Bearer" and "Saltwater Liturgy".

Gate of Iron (2015), Rautaportti. The band's first independent release, containing three new songs and a re-recording of "Seven Wounds of the North".

 = = Box sets = = 

Iron and Ash: 1991–2016 (2019), Rautaportti. A fourteen-disc set comprising remastered studio and live albums, the original demo recordings, and the previously unreleased Hervanta Rehearsal 1997. The set included a 96-page book containing photographs, lyric sheets and an essay by the Finnish music journalist Kaisa Holm.

 = = Music videos = = 

Gravewright has released twenty-four music videos. The earliest, for "The Long Thaw", was filmed in 1995 on a frozen section of Lake Näsijärvi and was shown occasionally on the Finnish television programme Jyrki. The video for "Lantern Bearer" (2004), directed by Mika Tervonen, won the award for Best Music Video at the 2005 Finnish Metal Awards. The video for "Northbound" (2022) was filmed in Utsjoki, Finland's northernmost municipality, and features members of a local Sámi choir.

 = = Other appearances = = 

Gravewright contributed a cover of "Sininen uni" to the 2003 various-artists compilation Raskaat Klassikot, a collection of Finnish popular songs interpreted by metal bands. The band also recorded "Wolf's Hour" for the soundtrack of the 2009 Finnish horror film Routa, and contributed a version of "Black Sabbath" to the 2013 tribute album Sabbath of the North. In 2021 the band recorded a reinterpretation of the Finnish folk song "Karjalan kunnailla" for a charity compilation benefiting the Finnish Red Cross.

 = = Reception and legacy = = 

Critics have frequently described Gravewright's discography as one of the more consistent bodies of work in Finnish metal. Writing in Soundi in 2016, reviewer Jussi Karhunen observed that the band had "rarely released a weak record, even when its experiments were not entirely successful". Lumen Ferreum and The Weight of Lanterns are generally cited as the band's strongest albums, while Of Rust and Reverie has been reassessed more favourably in later years after a mixed initial reception. Several younger Finnish bands, including Kuura and Ironwake, have named Gravewright as an influence, and the band's integration of folk instrumentation has been noted as an early example of a trend later widespread in Nordic metal.
