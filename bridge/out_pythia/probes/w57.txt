Marcus Delacroix-Vane

Marcus Anthony Vane (born March 14, 1984) is an American professional wrestler, wrestling trainer, and occasional television commentator, best known by the ring name Marcus Delacroix. He is best known for his work with the Midwest-based promotion Heartland Championship Wrestling (HCW) and later with the Japanese promotion Kyokuto Pro Wrestling, where he became the first non-Japanese wrestler to win the Kyokuto Openweight Championship twice. He also had a run with the national promotion Apex Wrestling Federation (AWF) between 2013 and 2017.

Over his career Vane has held more than a dozen championships in various promotions, including the HCW Heavyweight Championship three times and the AWF Tag Team Championship once alongside Dominic Rourke as part of the team known as The Iron Covenant. He is noted for a technical, submission-based style that draws on his amateur wrestling background. Writers for several independent wrestling publications have named him one of the most consistent in-ring performers of his generation.

= = Early life = =

Vane was born in Toledo, Ohio, the second of three children of Gerald Vane, a machinist at a local automotive parts plant, and Lorraine Delacroix-Vane, a public school music teacher. His maternal grandfather, Henri Delacroix, had emigrated from Quebec to Michigan in the 1950s, and Vane later adopted his mother's maiden name as part of his ring name in tribute to him.

Vane attended Whitmer High School, where he competed in amateur wrestling and football. As a senior in the 2001–02 season he placed third in the Ohio state championships in the 189-pound weight class. He received a partial athletic scholarship to Bowling Green State University. He wrestled for two seasons before a torn labrum in his left shoulder ended his collegiate career. He studied kinesiology and later said in interviews that his time in physical therapy after the injury got him interested in the physical side of professional wrestling.

Vane has said that he watched professional wrestling regularly as a child, citing televised broadcasts of territorial promotions and later national cable programming. He named Japanese and British technical wrestlers he saw on traded videotapes as his main stylistic influences.

= = Professional wrestling career = =

= = = Training and early career (2005–2008) = = =

In the summer of 2005 Vane left Bowling Green State University and enrolled at the Great Lakes Wrestling Academy in Dearborn, Michigan. The school was run by veteran wrestler Clayton "Bulldog" Pruitt. Vane trained there for roughly eleven months and later said that Pruitt, a former amateur wrestler himself, encouraged him to build his in-ring style around the grappling skills he already had rather than imitate the more theatrical styles popular on television at the time.

Vane made his professional debut on April 22, 2006, at a Great Lakes Wrestling Academy showcase event in Dearborn, losing to fellow trainee Shane Corliss. He wrestled for the first year under the name "Marc Vane." He worked primarily for small independent promotions in Michigan, Ohio, and Indiana, often on cards held in high school gymnasiums, county fairgrounds, and veterans' halls.

In 2007 Vane began using the name Marcus Delacroix at the suggestion of promoter Ronnie Tibbs of Northwest Ohio Championship Wrestling (NOCW). Under the new name he was given a gimmick as a disciplined, somewhat aloof technician. He won the NOCW Heavyweight Championship on November 10, 2007, defeating "Wild" Jake Harlan in a two-out-of-three falls match. He held the title for 214 days before losing it to Harlan in a rematch in June 2008.

= = = Heartland Championship Wrestling (2008–2013) = = =

In mid-2008 Vane signed a part-time agreement with Heartland Championship Wrestling, a promotion based in Indianapolis that ran monthly events across Indiana, Illinois, and Kentucky and produced a weekly syndicated television program. He debuted on August 16, 2008, defeating Eli Stroud in a preliminary match.

Over his first year with the company he was booked in a series of matches against the promotion's technical wrestlers. His feud with Tyrone "The Technician" Bell drew attention in the regional wrestling press. The two wrestled a 45-minute match at HCW Harvest Brawl in October 2009 that ended in a time-limit draw. The newsletter The Ringside Ledger later named it the best match in a Midwestern independent promotion that year.

On March 6, 2010, Vane defeated Bell to win the HCW Heavyweight Championship for the first time at HCW Spring Thaw. During his 302-day reign he defended the title against wrestlers from other independent promotions as part of HCW's "Open Border" series. He lost the championship to Big Ezra Dunmore on January 2, 2011, in a match marred by interference from Dunmore's stable, the Dunmore Family.

The loss began Vane's longest-running storyline in HCW, in which he formed an alliance with former rivals Tyrone Bell and Danny Okafor to oppose the Dunmore Family. The feud culminated at HCW Summer Siege 2011 in a steel cage match, which Vane's team won. He regained the HCW Heavyweight Championship from Dunmore on September 17, 2011, and held it until April 2012, when he lost it to Okafor in a match that turned Okafor into a villain.

Vane won the title for a third time in December 2012. Shortly afterward he announced that he would be leaving the promotion after signing a contract with the Apex Wrestling Federation. He lost the title to Bell in his final HCW match on February 23, 2013. The two embraced in the ring afterward, which several hundred attendees met with a standing ovation.

= = = Apex Wrestling Federation (2013–2017) = = =

= = = = Developmental and debut (2013–2014) = = = =

Vane reported to the AWF's developmental territory, AWF Forge, in Orlando, Florida, in March 2013. Because of his extensive independent experience he was used both as a wrestler and as an informal on-site mentor for newer trainees. He wrestled under his own name in Forge and competed in the promotion's inaugural Forge Classic tournament in 2013, reaching the final before losing to Jaxon Reyes.

Vane made his main roster television debut on the AWF's weekly program Apex Live on June 9, 2014, defeating Lucas Montague. AWF officials initially presented him as an aristocratic villain, complete with a ring jacket and a valet named Celeste Marchand, played by former fitness competitor Brianna Holt. Critics generally found the character a poor fit for Vane's understated delivery. In a 2019 interview he said, "I was a guy from Toledo who liked to put people in armbars, and they had me drinking from a wine glass on the entrance ramp. It just wasn't me."

= = = = The Iron Covenant (2015–2016) = = = =

In early 2015 the AWF dropped the aristocratic character and paired Vane with Dominic Rourke, a former mixed martial arts competitor, as a tag team called The Iron Covenant. The team was presented as a pair of no-nonsense grapplers, and the change drew a noticeably better response from audiences. The two defeated The Sterling Brothers to win the AWF Tag Team Championship on August 23, 2015, at the pay-per-view event Apex Summerfest.

The Iron Covenant held the championships for 168 days, making successful defenses against teams including The Sterling Brothers, Midnight Express 2.0, and the team of Kenji Arata and Malik Townsend. They lost the titles to The Bastion, the team of Greg Holloway and Tre Vickers, on February 7, 2016. Rourke suffered a knee injury in April 2016 that kept him out of competition for most of the year, and the team was quietly dissolved.

= = = = Singles run and departure (2016–2017) = = = =

After Rourke's injury Vane returned to singles competition. He took part in a lengthy feud with Jaxon Reyes, his former Forge rival, during the second half of 2016. Their match at Apex Fall Fury 2016, which Reyes won by submission after nearly thirty minutes, received positive reviews and was the highest-rated match of the event in several published reader polls.

Vane challenged for the AWF Intercontinental Heritage Championship twice in early 2017 but did not win the title. By mid-2017 he was appearing less often on television. He requested his release in July 2017, and the AWF granted it on August 4, 2017. In later interviews Vane described his departure as amicable, saying he had wanted to wrestle a heavier schedule than the company could offer him at the time.

= = = Kyokuto Pro Wrestling (2017–2023) = = =

Vane made his debut for Tokyo-based Kyokuto Pro Wrestling on October 14, 2017, as a participant in the promotion's annual Autumn Crown tournament, a round-robin competition. Although he did not win the tournament, he finished with a record of five wins and three losses and defeated two former Kyokuto Openweight Champions, Ryota Kanemura and Shinjiro Hayakawa. These results led Kyokuto to sign him to a full-time contract beginning in January 2018.

Under Kyokuto's booking Vane joined the faction Reign of Steel, an alliance of foreign and Japanese wrestlers led by veteran Hiroshi Takeda. He spent most of 2018 competing in tag team and trios matches with the group. Japanese wrestling magazines praised his adjustment to the promotion's hard-hitting style. He and Takeda won the Kyokuto Tag Team Championship in July 2018 and held it until January 2019.

On May 3, 2019, at Kyokuto Golden Climax, Vane defeated Ryota Kanemura to win the Kyokuto Openweight Championship. He became the third non-Japanese wrestler in the promotion's history to hold the title. His reign lasted 287 days and included eight successful defenses, among them a 52-minute match with Shinjiro Hayakawa in December 2019. Several Japanese and English-language publications named it match of the year. He lost the championship to Daichi Moriyama on February 14, 2020.

The COVID-19 pandemic interrupted Kyokuto's schedule in 2020, and Vane stayed in Japan for most of the period when the promotion ran events without spectators. He later said that this period, in which he wrestled frequently in front of empty arenas, was "the strangest but in some ways most educational" part of his career. He was one of only a few foreign wrestlers who continued to appear for the promotion throughout 2020.

Vane left Reign of Steel in a storyline in early 2021 after Takeda attacked him following a tag team loss. The resulting feud between the two former partners ended at Kyokuto Grand Destiny 2021, where Vane defeated Takeda in a no-disqualification match. He won the Autumn Crown tournament in November 2021, defeating Moriyama in the final. This earned him a championship match at the promotion's January 4, 2022 event, Kyokuto New Year Dome.

At that event Vane defeated Moriyama to win the Kyokuto Openweight Championship a second time, becoming the first non-Japanese wrestler to hold the title on two separate occasions. His second reign lasted 196 days, ending when he lost to the rising wrestler Kaito Shirasaki on July 19, 2022. Vane remained with the promotion for another year, working primarily in an upper-midcard role and helping to establish younger wrestlers. His last full-time Kyokuto appearance came on September 10, 2023, when he lost to Shirasaki in what was billed as his farewell match in the promotion.

= = = Return to independent circuit (2023–present) = = = =

After leaving Japan Vane returned to the United States and began working a limited schedule on the independent circuit. He made a highly publicized return to Heartland Championship Wrestling on November 18, 2023, more than a decade after his departure, to confront then-champion Danny Okafor. The two wrestled at HCW's 25th anniversary event in March 2024, where Okafor retained the championship.

Since 2024 Vane has appeared for several independent promotions in the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom, typically in special attraction matches. He has also made occasional appearances for Kyokuto Pro Wrestling as part of the promotion's international tours. He has said that he intends to retire from full-time competition by the end of 2026, though he has not ruled out appearing in occasional matches afterward.

= = Professional wrestling style and persona = =

Vane's in-ring style is generally described as technical and submission-based, drawing heavily on his amateur wrestling background. He commonly uses joint locks, mat-based holds, and suplex variations. His primary finishing maneuver, called the Toledo Stretch, is a modified Fujiwara armbar applied from a seated position while the opponent's legs are trapped. During his Kyokuto tenure he added a high-angle bridging German suplex, called the Delacroix Bridge, as a secondary finisher.

Commentators have often described his presentation as understated and businesslike. Aside from his brief aristocratic character in the AWF, Vane has mostly wrestled as a heroic character, though he played a villain at various points in HCW and Kyokuto. He typically wrestles in plain black trunks and kneepads, a look he has said was inspired by the Japanese and British wrestlers he watched as a teenager.

= = Other media = =

Vane appeared as a guest color commentator on Kyokuto's English-language broadcasts on several occasions between 2020 and 2023 while recovering from minor injuries. Viewers noted his detailed explanations of submission holds. He co-hosts a podcast, The Mat Room, with former HCW announcer Phil Garrity, which began in 2021 and features interviews with wrestlers and trainers.

He appears as a playable character in the video game Kyokuto Pro Wrestling: Fighting Spirit (2021) and its 2023 sequel. He also appeared briefly in the 2022 independent documentary Gym Floors and Fairgrounds, which follows wrestlers working on the Midwestern independent circuit.

= = Training career = =

In 2024 Vane opened the Delacroix Grappling Academy in Toledo, Ohio, a professional wrestling school that also offers amateur wrestling and submission grappling classes. The school emphasizes fundamentals and mat wrestling. Vane has said he wanted to create a place where students could learn "the old way," with heavy focus on conditioning and technique before performance. Several of the school's graduates have since debuted on the Midwestern independent circuit, and two signed developmental contracts with national promotions in 2025.

= = Personal life = =

Vane married his longtime partner, Rachel Okonkwo, a physical therapist, in 2016. The couple have one daughter, born in 2019. They live in the Toledo area.

Vane has spoken publicly about the physical toll of his career, including surgeries on both shoulders and his right knee. He has advocated for better health care access for independent wrestlers and has taken part in fundraising events for wrestlers without insurance. He is a lifelong supporter of the Toledo Mud Hens minor league baseball team and has thrown the ceremonial first pitch at two of their games.
