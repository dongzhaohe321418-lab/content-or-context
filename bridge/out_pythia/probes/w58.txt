Wenslow and Brackford is a constituency in the county of Northamptonshire, represented in the House of Commons of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It was created for the 1983 general election and was abolished in 2024. It was re-established with altered boundaries in the same year. It elects one Member of Parliament (MP) by the first-past-the-post system of election. Since 2024 the seat has been held by Helen Marchbank of the Labour Party.

The constituency covers the market town of Wenslow, the smaller town of Brackford, and a group of rural parishes in the valley of the River Ise and its tributaries. Historically it was considered a bellwether seat. It changed hands between the Conservative and Labour parties at most changes of government between 1983 and 2024.

= = History = =

= = = Predecessor seats = = =

Before 1983 the area now covered by Wenslow and Brackford was split between two constituencies. Wenslow and its surrounding parishes formed part of the Mid Northamptonshire division, created under the Redistribution of Seats Act 1885. Brackford lay within the Kettering division for most of the twentieth century. The Mid Northamptonshire seat was abolished in 1918. Wenslow was then placed in the Daventry division until 1950, and afterwards in a county constituency known as Ise Valley.

Ise Valley was regarded as a safe Conservative seat for much of its existence. It was held from 1950 to 1970 by Sir Walter Ingram-Lyle, a former officer in the Royal Northamptonshire Yeomanry. He sat on the backbenches throughout his parliamentary career and took a particular interest in agricultural policy. His successor, Geoffrey Pellow, held the seat until its abolition. Pellow then retired from the Commons.

= = = Creation = = =

The Boundary Commission for England began its Third Periodical Review in 1976. It proposed a new constituency combining Wenslow, Brackford and the rural district between them. The commission noted that Wenslow had grown considerably since the 1950s, largely because of overspill housing built for families moving from London. As a result, the electorate of Ise Valley had risen well above the county quota.

The initial proposal used the name "Wenslow". Following a local inquiry held at Brackford Corn Exchange in 1981, the commission adopted the name "Wenslow and Brackford". Representatives of Brackford Town Council had argued that the town's separate identity should be recognised. The new seat was first contested at the 1983 general election.

= = = 1983–1997 = = =

The first MP for Wenslow and Brackford was Conservative Roger Tennant, a solicitor and former leader of Northamptonshire County Council. He won the seat in 1983 with a majority of 9,412 over Labour, while the SDP–Liberal Alliance came a close third. Tennant increased his majority in 1987.

He served as a parliamentary private secretary at the Department of Transport between 1988 and 1990. He was closely associated with campaigns for a bypass around Wenslow, which was opened in 1993. In 1992 his majority fell to 3,106. Analysts attributed the decline to the closure of the Hallam & Doyle footwear factory in Brackford, which had been the town's largest employer.

= = = 1997–2010 = = =

At the 1997 general election, Labour candidate Dennis Okafor gained the seat on a swing of 11.8 per cent. Okafor was a former maths teacher and Wenslow borough councillor, and the first Black MP elected in Northamptonshire. He held the seat in 2001 and 2005, although his majority was reduced at each election.

In Parliament, Okafor sat on the Education and Skills Select Committee. He promoted a private member's bill on school transport in rural areas, which did not proceed beyond its second reading. He became known locally for opposing the proposed downgrading of the maternity unit at Wenslow General Hospital in 2004. The unit was eventually retained after a review by the strategic health authority.

= = = 2010–2024 = = =

The Fifth Periodical Review made minor alterations to the seat's boundaries ahead of the 2010 general election. The rural ward of Kelmarsh Heath was transferred to the neighbouring Daventry constituency. In exchange, Wenslow and Brackford gained the Orton and Loddington ward from Kettering.

Okafor stood down at the election. Conservative candidate Philippa Arden-Hughes gained the seat with a majority of 5,887. Arden-Hughes was a former management consultant. She was re-elected in 2015, 2017 and 2019.

From 2016 to 2018 she served as a junior minister in the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs. She resigned in July 2018 in protest at the government's proposed withdrawal agreement with the European Union. She later rejoined the government as a whip in 2019. Arden-Hughes announced in 2023 that she would not seek re-election.

In the 2016 referendum on the United Kingdom's membership of the European Union, the area covered by the constituency was estimated to have voted to leave by a margin of approximately 61 per cent to 39 per cent.

= = = Abolition and re-creation = = =

The 2023 Periodic Review of Westminster constituencies initially proposed dividing Wenslow and Brackford between three new seats. The proposal attracted significant local opposition. More than 2,400 written representations were submitted to the Boundary Commission, the majority of them objecting to the separation of Wenslow from Brackford.

In its revised proposals, the commission recommended a reconstituted Wenslow and Brackford constituency. Its boundaries were broadly similar to those of its predecessor. The northern parishes of Rushden Wold and Great Harrow were removed, and the Hensby ward from the former Corby constituency was added. Because the change in electorate exceeded the commission's threshold, the seat was formally treated as a new constituency.

At the 2024 general election, Labour candidate Helen Marchbank won the seat with a majority of 2,941. Reform UK finished in third place with 19.4 per cent of the vote.

The constituency originally comprised the following wards and parishes:

* The Borough of Wenslow wards of Abbey, Castle Hill, Fenwick, Mill Lane, Northgate, St Barnabas and Westfield.
* The District of East Ise wards of Brackford Central, Brackford North, Brackford South, Harrowden, Kelmarsh Heath, Rushden Wold and Thornwell.

* The Borough of Wenslow wards of Abbey, Castle Hill, Fenwick, Mill Lane, Northgate, St Barnabas and Westfield.
* The District of East Ise wards of Brackford Central, Brackford North, Brackford South, Harrowden, Orton and Loddington, Rushden Wold and Thornwell.

= = = 2024–present = = =

Wenslow and East Ise were merged into the unitary authority of North Northamptonshire in 2021, so the current boundaries are defined by the wards of that authority:

* Brackford, Hensby, Ise Valley, Orton, Thornwell, Wenslow Central, Wenslow East and Wenslow West.

The constituency is bordered by Daventry to the west, Kettering to the east, Corby and East Northamptonshire to the north, and Wellingborough and Rushden to the south.

= = Constituency profile = =

Wenslow is the largest settlement in the constituency, with a population of approximately 46,000 at the 2021 census. The town developed as a market centre in the medieval period and received a royal charter for a weekly market in 1263. Its economy was historically based on leather tanning and the manufacture of boots and shoes, as well as brewing. The Wenslow Brewery operated on its Northgate site from 1794 until 1988. The brewery building has since been converted into apartments and a small museum.

After the Second World War, Wenslow was designated as an expanded town under the Town Development Act 1952. Several large housing estates were built in the 1960s and 1970s, mainly in the Fenwick and Westfield areas. Since the 1990s the town's economy has shifted towards logistics and distribution. A number of large warehouses have been built near the junction of the Wenslow bypass with the A43.

Brackford has a population of around 14,500. It was historically known for footwear manufacturing, in common with many towns in the county. The closure of the Hallam & Doyle works in 1991 led to a prolonged period of high unemployment in the town. The site was redeveloped in the early 2000s as the Doyle's Yard retail and residential scheme. Brackford retains a number of listed buildings, including the fourteenth-century Church of St Giles and the Georgian Corn Exchange.

The rural part of the constituency is predominantly arable farmland, with several villages of ironstone construction. Harrowden is the largest village and is known for its annual ploughing match, held since 1872. Thornwell Reservoir was constructed in 1958 to supply water to the expanding towns. It is now a site of special scientific interest owing to its overwintering bird populations.

At the 2021 census, the constituency had a slightly higher proportion of residents aged 65 and over than the national average. Home ownership rates were also above the national average. The proportion of residents holding degree-level qualifications was below the average for England. Wenslow's Fenwick ward is among the 20 per cent most deprived areas in England according to the English Indices of Deprivation, while several rural wards rank among the least deprived.

= = Political history = =

Wenslow and Brackford has generally been regarded as a marginal seat. Labour support has traditionally been concentrated in the Fenwick, Westfield and Northgate areas of Wenslow and in central Brackford. The Conservative vote has been strongest in the rural wards and in the more affluent Castle Hill area of Wenslow.

The Liberal Democrats and their predecessors achieved their best result in 1983, when the SDP–Liberal Alliance won 27.3 per cent of the vote. Their share declined at subsequent elections. In 2015 the Liberal Democrat candidate lost their deposit for the first time.

The UK Independence Party (UKIP) took 16.8 per cent of the vote in 2015, finishing in third place. The party did not stand in 2019. Its successor in electoral terms, Reform UK, achieved a substantially stronger result in 2024.

At local level, North Northamptonshire Council wards within the constituency were held predominantly by Conservative councillors following the authority's first elections in 2021. Labour held the Fenwick and Brackford Central wards, and an independent councillor represented Thornwell.

= = Notable events = =

The 2005 general election count in Wenslow and Brackford attracted national attention. A recount was requested by the Conservative candidate after the initial result showed a Labour majority of fewer than 900 votes. The recount was completed at 6:40 a.m. at Wenslow Leisure Centre and confirmed Okafor's re-election with a slightly increased majority of 1,244.

In 2012, Arden-Hughes led a cross-party campaign with neighbouring MPs to reopen Wenslow railway station, which had closed to passengers in 1964 under the Beeching cuts. A feasibility study commissioned by Northamptonshire County Council in 2014 concluded that reopening the station would be technically possible but would need significant government funding. As of 2024 no funding had been allocated, although the proposal remained part of the local transport plan.

During the 2019 general election campaign, a hustings event at Brackford Corn Exchange was abandoned after a fire alarm was triggered. It was rescheduled two days later at St Giles' Church Hall.
