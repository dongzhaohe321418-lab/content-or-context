The 1987 Wattle Ranges Football League season was the 42nd season of the Wattle Ranges Football League, a fictional Australian rules football competition based in the inland Victorian towns of the Wattle River district. Ten clubs contested an eighteen-round home-and-away season, followed by a four-week finals series. The season began on 11 April and concluded on 19 September, when East Wattle defeated Carrawarra by 17 points in the grand final at the Wattle Showgrounds.

East Wattle won its sixth senior premiership and its first since 1978. Carrawarra finished first on the home-and-away ladder with fifteen wins but lost both of its finals. Ranges United, which qualified in fifth place, won three successive elimination matches before losing the preliminary final to East Wattle.

The season was the league’s first under a five-team finals system. It also featured the introduction of a central player-registration register, revised arrangements for gate receipts and a trial of two field umpires in selected senior matches. Persistent rain during June and July affected several grounds, although every scheduled senior match was completed.

= = Background = =

The Wattle Ranges Football League was established in 1946 by six clubs from agricultural and railway settlements along the Wattle River. Its membership increased during the following three decades as roads improved and neighbouring district competitions amalgamated. The admission of Ironbark and Lake Marrin in 1979 brought the competition to ten clubs.

By the mid-1980s, the league encompassed a district extending approximately 110 kilometres from Glenross in the west to North Junction in the east. Most clubs fielded senior, reserve and under-17 teams. Clubs generally drew players from their immediate townships and surrounding farms, supplemented by recruits travelling from larger regional centres.

Carrawarra entered 1987 as the reigning premier, having defeated Stonebridge in the 1986 grand final. East Wattle had finished fourth in that season and lost the first semi-final. Bellfield, the 1985 premier, was rebuilding following the retirement of several experienced players.

Discussions about the competition’s financial position occupied much of the preceding summer. Three clubs reported operating deficits, while rising transport and insurance costs reduced the amount available for ground improvements. The league rejected a proposal to divide the competition into geographical sections, retaining the existing arrangement under which every club played each opponent twice.

= = Administration = =

The league’s annual meeting was held at the Carrawarra Mechanics’ Institute on 26 November 1986. Harold Venn was re-elected president for a fourth term. Margaret Telford continued as secretary, while former Stonebridge treasurer Colin Rusk became league treasurer.

Delegates approved the replacement of the four-team finals series with a five-team system by seven votes to three. Supporters argued that the change would maintain interest among middle-ranking clubs and provide an additional finals gate. Opponents considered that allowing half the competition to qualify would diminish the importance of the home-and-away season.

Under the revised system, the minor premier received a bye in the opening week of the finals. The second- and third-placed teams contested a qualifying final, while the fourth- and fifth-placed teams played an elimination final. The remaining matches followed the usual sequence of semi-finals, a preliminary final and a grand final.

A central registration register replaced separate club lists maintained throughout the season. Clubs were required to lodge player registrations before noon on the Friday preceding a player’s first appearance. The arrangement followed two eligibility disputes in 1986, neither of which had resulted in a change to match results.

The league also introduced a standard match-report form covering injuries, reports and ground conditions. Club secretaries submitted the forms with their score sheets on the Monday following each round.

= = Participating clubs = =

The ten clubs competing in 1987 were Bellfield, Carrawarra, East Wattle, Glenross, Ironbark, Lake Marrin, North Junction, Ranges United, South Wattle and Stonebridge.

Carrawarra retained most of its premiership side under playing coach Peter Haldane. East Wattle appointed former district representative captain Malcolm Reeve as non-playing coach, allowing midfielder Alan Pritchard to relinquish coaching duties while remaining captain.

Stonebridge continued under coach Denis Vale. Its principal recruit was ruckman Stephen Alder, who returned to the district after four seasons in a coastal competition. North Junction appointed teacher and former Glenross player Brian Morrow as captain-coach.

Ranges United, formed through the merger of Upper Wattle and Mount Ranges in 1968, recruited several younger players from its under-17 premiership team. Bellfield lost its leading defender, Graham Pell, to retirement but retained forward Russell Pike.

South Wattle appointed a new committee after recording a financial loss in 1986. Glenross relied heavily on locally developed players, while Lake Marrin recruited midfielder Colin Nairn from an adjoining league. Ironbark entered the season with the competition’s youngest senior squad.

All clubs used their established home grounds. Ranges United divided its home matches between the Upper Wattle Recreation Reserve and Mount Ranges Oval, continuing an arrangement dating from the merger.

= = Competition format = =

Each club played eighteen home-and-away matches, meeting every opponent once at home and once away. Four premiership points were awarded for a win and two for a draw. Teams level on points were separated by percentage, calculated by dividing total points scored by total points conceded and multiplying the result by one hundred.

Senior matches consisted of four quarters of twenty-five minutes plus time-on. Reserve and under-17 matches were played before the senior fixture wherever ground conditions permitted.

The fixture included a general bye on 13 June for the interleague match and another on 25 July. The second break was retained partly to accommodate agricultural shows and partly to provide an opportunity for postponed matches. No senior fixtures required rescheduling.

The league continued to use one field umpire for most home-and-away matches. A two-umpire system was trialled in eight fixtures and used throughout the senior finals. Boundary and goal umpires remained club-appointed during the regular season, with neutral appointments made for finals.

= = Pre-season = =

Practice matches began in March. East Wattle won all three of its scheduled fixtures, including a 28-point victory over Carrawarra. Reeve used the matches to establish a more settled defensive formation, with former centre half-forward David Linton moved to centre half-back.

Carrawarra’s preparation was interrupted by injuries to rover Michael Searle and defender Anthony Breen. Both returned before the opening round, although Searle played limited minutes during April.

North Junction attracted attention after defeating Stonebridge in a practice match at the Junction Recreation Reserve. Morrow introduced a shorter passing game and moved several experienced players into different positions.

The league abandoned its proposed pre-season lightning premiership after receiving only six entries. Clubs cited concerns about player availability during the late harvest and the cost of preparing grounds. A junior coaching day was held instead at the Wattle Showgrounds.

= = Home-and-away season = =

Carrawarra opened the season with six consecutive victories. Its strongest early performance was a 71-point win over Bellfield in round four, when full-forward Trevor Dacey kicked nine goals. East Wattle won five of its first six matches, its only defeat coming against Stonebridge.

Ironbark recorded its first victory in round five, defeating Lake Marrin by three points. The match was decided by a goal from nineteen-year-old forward Mark Heskett late in the final quarter. Ironbark subsequently lost eight consecutive matches before beating Glenross in round fourteen.

The first draw occurred in round seven, when Ranges United and Lake Marrin both finished on 84 points. Lake Marrin led by nineteen points at three-quarter time but conceded four goals in the final term. A shot by Ranges United captain Neil Travers struck the post shortly before the siren.

Carrawarra’s unbeaten start ended in round seven with a nine-point loss to North Junction. Morrow’s side restricted Dacey to two goals and scored five goals in the third quarter while kicking with a strong wind.

East Wattle moved to the top of the ladder after round nine. Its 34-point victory over Carrawarra at East Wattle Oval was regarded locally as its best performance of the first half of the season. Pritchard and wingman Simon Keast provided repeated entries into attack, while Linton held Carrawarra centre half-forward Paul Reddin goalless.

Heavy rain affected rounds ten to thirteen. Glenross Oval became particularly difficult to use, and the reserve match before Glenross’s round-eleven meeting with Bellfield was transferred to a school ground. The senior match proceeded, with Bellfield winning 5.12 (42) to 4.9 (33).

Stonebridge remained in the top three throughout the middle of the season. Alder’s ruck work and the consistent scoring of forward Ian Bostock helped compensate for injuries to its midfield. North Junction fluctuated between fourth and sixth before winning four matches in succession during July and August.

The second draw was played in round thirteen, when South Wattle and Glenross each scored 9.12 (66). South Wattle had led by seven goals during the second quarter. Glenross’s recovery included five unanswered goals in the third term.

Ranges United entered the final three rounds in sixth place, behind Bellfield on percentage. It defeated South Wattle and Stonebridge before meeting Bellfield in the final round. Ranges United won that match by 22 points, securing fifth position and ending Bellfield’s finals hopes.

Carrawarra regained first place in round sixteen and retained it with victories over Lake Marrin and Glenross. East Wattle finished one win behind. Stonebridge placed third, followed by North Junction and Ranges United.

= = Ladder = =

Carrawarra finished with fifteen wins and three losses for 60 points and a percentage of 145.5. East Wattle recorded fourteen wins and four losses for 56 points and a percentage of 135.1.

Stonebridge finished third with twelve wins and six losses, earning 48 points and a percentage of 120.0. North Junction placed fourth with eleven wins and seven losses, also finishing with a percentage of 120.0 but four fewer premiership points.

Ranges United qualified fifth with nine wins, eight losses and one draw for 38 points and a percentage of 104.0. Bellfield finished sixth with eight wins and ten losses, earning 32 points and a percentage of 95.0.

South Wattle recorded six wins, eleven losses and one draw for 26 points and a percentage of 87.5. Glenross finished with five wins, twelve losses and one draw for 22 points and a percentage of 80.0.

Lake Marrin placed ninth with four wins, thirteen losses and one draw for 18 points and a percentage of 71.4. Ironbark finished last with four wins and fourteen losses for 16 points and a percentage of 70.0.

= = Finals series = =

The finals began on 29 August. East Wattle defeated Stonebridge in the qualifying final at North Junction, scoring 14.12 (96) to 11.10 (76). East Wattle established a four-goal lead during the second quarter and maintained an advantage despite Stonebridge reducing the margin to eight points early in the final term.

Ranges United defeated North Junction in the elimination final at Bellfield the following day. The final score was 12.15 (87) to 12.9 (81). North Junction led for most of the match, but Ranges United kicked the last three goals.

In the first semi-final, Ranges United beat Stonebridge 13.8 (86) to 10.13 (73). Travers played much of the second half at full-forward and kicked four goals. Stonebridge’s inaccurate shooting contributed to its second successive defeat and elimination.

East Wattle defeated Carrawarra in the second semi-final by 26 points, 15.11 (101) to 11.9 (75). Carrawarra led at half-time before East Wattle scored seven goals in the third quarter. The result gave East Wattle direct entry to the grand final.

Ranges United’s finals run ended in the preliminary final, where Carrawarra won 16.10 (106) to 11.14 (80). Dacey kicked six goals. Ranges United remained within twelve points at the final change but conceded the first three goals of the last quarter.

= = Grand final = =

The grand final was played at the Wattle Showgrounds on Saturday 19 September before an estimated crowd of 4,250. Conditions were dry, with a light north-westerly wind. Both clubs selected unchanged teams from their previous finals.

Carrawarra began strongly and led by eleven points at quarter-time. Dacey kicked two early goals, while Haldane and Searle controlled several centre clearances. East Wattle’s first-quarter score included five behinds, three from set shots.

East Wattle improved during the second quarter. Pritchard moved from the centre to ruck-rover, and Keast began playing closer to the contest. East Wattle kicked five goals to two for the quarter and led by nine points at half-time.

The third quarter produced only three goals. Linton intercepted several Carrawarra attacks, while East Wattle full-back Robert Fenner limited Dacey’s opportunities. East Wattle extended its lead to fourteen points at the final change.

Carrawarra reduced the margin to two points early in the last quarter through goals from Reddin and Searle. East Wattle responded with three consecutive goals, including two from forward Peter Noll. A late Carrawarra goal narrowed the margin, but East Wattle retained possession through much of the closing period.

East Wattle won 13.16 (94) to 11.11 (77). Noll kicked four goals and half-forward Darren Wicks three. Dacey finished with four for Carrawarra.

Pritchard received the medal for best player in the grand final. Linton, Keast and Fenner were also prominent for East Wattle. Haldane was named Carrawarra’s best player.

= = Awards = =

North Junction captain-coach Brian Morrow won the league’s best-and-fairest award, the A. J. Mercer Medal, with 24 votes. East Wattle captain Alan Pritchard finished second with 21, followed by Stonebridge ruckman Stephen Alder on 19.

Votes were awarded by field umpires on a three-two-one basis after each home-and-away match. The count was held at the Bellfield Memorial Hall on 7 September. Morrow became North Junction’s first winner since 1972.

Trevor Dacey won the senior goalkicking award with 86 goals during the home-and-away season. Bellfield’s Russell Pike finished second with 71, ahead of East Wattle’s Peter Noll on 64. Dacey added thirteen goals during the finals.

Ironbark midfielder Andrew Sloane received the first-year player award. The league’s club administration award went to Lake Marrin, which completed the season without a late registration, missing score sheet or financial penalty.

= = Representative football = =

The Wattle Ranges representative side played the neighbouring Redgum Plains league at Stonebridge on 13 June. Wattle Ranges won 17.12 (114) to 14.15 (99), ending a run of three defeats in the annual fixture.

Malcolm Reeve coached the side, with Peter Haldane serving as captain. Brian Morrow withdrew because of a minor hamstring injury. Dacey kicked five goals, while Alder and Pritchard were among the leading players.

The fixture drew approximately 1,300 spectators. Gate proceeds were divided between the host club, the league and the district hospital appeal.

= = Reserves and junior competitions = =

Stonebridge won the reserve premiership, defeating East Wattle 10.9 (69) to 8.11 (59). Stonebridge had finished second on the ladder and won three finals.

Bellfield won the under-17 premiership with a 23-point victory over Carrawarra. Bellfield midfielder Jason Orr received the junior best-and-fairest award.

Ironbark and Lake Marrin occasionally combined under-17 players for non-premiership practice matches when numbers were insufficient, but maintained separate teams for the official competition.

= = Aftermath = =

The league reported increased finals revenue, attributing the improvement partly to the additional match and Ranges United’s extended participation. Clubs voted to retain the five-team finals system for 1988.

The two-umpire trial received broad support, although a shortage of accredited officials prevented its immediate introduction across every senior match. The league instead expanded the arrangement gradually.

East Wattle’s premiership was followed by a civic reception and a club gathering at its recreation reserve. Reeve was reappointed for 1988, while Pritchard remained captain. Carrawarra retained Haldane despite its consecutive finals defeats.

Ground drainage and junior player numbers remained the principal matters identified for the following season. The league established a small grounds committee and began discussions with schools about a district junior development programme.
