The Tide Ledger

The Tide Ledger (Japanese: 潮の帳簿, Hepburn: Shio no Chōbo) is a Japanese television drama series written by Haruka Minase and directed by Kōhei Sugita. It was broadcast by the fictional regional network Tōkai Central Television in ten episodes from 9 October to 11 December 2014. Set in the coastal city of Mizunoe, the series follows a municipal archivist investigating discrepancies in records relating to a harbour redevelopment scheme. Its principal cast includes Nao Serizawa as archivist Tomoko Saeki, Masato Kirihara as harbour engineer Shūji Maki, and Yūko Naruse as fish merchant Fumie Saeki.

The series combines a family drama with an investigation into land ownership, public administration, and the decline of a commercial fishing port. Each episode centres on a document whose interpretation changes the characters’ understanding of a disputed waterfront district. The investigation develops through interviews, routine administrative work, and comparisons between records maintained by different institutions.

Produced by Kurohama Pictures, The Tide Ledger was filmed principally in coastal Shizuoka Prefecture. It attracted a modest audience during its original broadcast and received favourable reviews for its ensemble performances and depiction of municipal employment. A television special, The Tide Ledger: Spring Soundings, was broadcast in March 2016.

= = Synopsis = =

Tomoko Saeki returns to Mizunoe after eleven years in Nagoya to take a temporary position in the municipal records office. Her father, a retired harbour pilot, has recently died, and her mother Fumie is struggling to maintain the family’s fish stall. Tomoko intends to sell her share of the family house and leave when her six-month contract ends.

Her first assignment is to prepare older harbour records for transfer to a new storage facility. Among the papers is a compensation ledger from 1978 containing several payments to households that do not appear in the corresponding residential register. The entries concern the East Basin, a district scheduled for redevelopment as a passenger terminal and shopping complex.

Tomoko initially assumes that the discrepancy is a transcription error. Harbour engineer Shūji Maki tells her that different agencies historically used different boundaries for the district. However, she subsequently discovers that a map cited in the compensation proceedings is missing from the city’s official collection. A photograph of the map appears in a local school commemorative booklet, showing a public access route omitted from later surveys.

As Tomoko traces the alteration, she learns that her father helped negotiate the original compensation settlement. The inquiry places her in conflict with her mother, former harbour employees, and municipal officials who regard the redevelopment as the city’s last opportunity to reverse its economic decline.

The series gradually establishes that the disputed records resulted from several overlapping decisions. Temporary housing was excluded from one survey, a footpath was treated as an industrial service road, and compensation was distributed through an informal residents’ association. Although some participants benefited financially, others accepted the arrangement to secure replacement housing before winter.

The final episodes concern whether the city should suspend redevelopment while the records are reviewed. Tomoko presents a report that distinguishes established facts from unresolved claims. The municipal assembly authorises a limited postponement and an independent land survey. The series ends before a final decision on the redevelopment, with Tomoko accepting a permanent archival post and beginning to catalogue records donated by former East Basin residents.

= = Cast and characters = =

Nao Serizawa portrays Tomoko Saeki, a 33-year-old archivist employed on a fixed-term municipal contract. Previously an assistant registrar at a private museum, Tomoko is familiar with cataloguing and conservation but inexperienced in local government. Her reluctance to discuss her father contrasts with her insistence that other residents explain their involvement in the harbour settlement.

Masato Kirihara plays Shūji Maki, an engineer in the city’s harbour maintenance division. Maki supports redevelopment because the existing sea defences require extensive repairs. He assists Tomoko with technical records while disputing her assumption that every inconsistency indicates misconduct. His departmental responsibilities become increasingly difficult to separate from his role in the investigation.

Yūko Naruse appears as Fumie Saeki, Tomoko’s mother and the proprietor of a fish stall in the covered market. Fumie considers the 1978 settlement a practical achievement that allowed several families to remain in Mizunoe. Her account provides an alternative to Tomoko’s initial interpretation of the documents.

Tatsurō Kume portrays Eizō Hoshina, the municipal records supervisor. Approaching retirement, Hoshina has developed an informal system for locating poorly catalogued material. He initially discourages Tomoko’s investigation but later admits that he retained duplicate papers after an earlier disposal order.

Other regular cast members include:

* Rika Hanabusa as Mika Endō, a local newspaper reporter covering municipal affairs.
* Junpei Arita as Kenta Saeki, Tomoko’s younger brother, who operates a refrigerated delivery van.
* Sachiko Moribe as Chie Nonomura, a former East Basin resident and retired school cook.
* Noboru Takase as Mayor Akira Fujishiro, a supporter of the redevelopment scheme.
* Keisuke Inami as Yōsuke Tsuji, a junior records clerk studying for the permanent civil service examination.
* Maki Tsunoda as Reiko Maki, Shūji’s estranged wife and a vocational school instructor.

Tomoko’s father, Isao Saeki, appears in brief flashbacks played by Ryōhei Matsuda. These sequences show domestic and working situations rather than directly reconstructing the disputed negotiations.

= = Episodes = =

The series consists of ten episodes, each approximately 46 minutes long. Episode titles refer to documents, filing practices, or physical traces found in the archive.

1. “The Unnumbered Box” introduces Tomoko’s return to Mizunoe and her discovery of the compensation ledger. A routine request to identify an unnamed recipient leads her to Chie Nonomura.
2. “Duplicate Copy” concerns two versions of a council memorandum. Maki explains the harbour’s changing administrative boundaries, while Fumie refuses permission to search Isao’s papers.
3. “A Line in Blue” follows the search for the missing survey map. A school booklet provides evidence of a waterfront path connecting the East Basin to the market.
4. “Notice of Removal” examines the relocation of temporary households. Kenta’s delivery contract with a redevelopment subcontractor complicates his support for Tomoko.
5. “Receipt Attached” reconstructs the distribution of compensation through the residents’ association. Chie acknowledges signing receipts on behalf of neighbours who could not attend meetings.
6. “The Closed Register” centres on restrictions governing personal records. Tomoko and Mika disagree over whether the newspaper should publish names before the payments have been explained.
7. “Water Damage” follows a storm that floods a harbour storage shed. During the recovery operation, Maki finds inspection papers documenting earlier concerns about the sea wall.
8. “For Permanent Retention” reveals Hoshina’s role in preserving duplicate files. Fumie gives Tomoko access to Isao’s notebooks, which contain household expenses alongside meeting notes.
9. “Corrections in Red” depicts the preparation of Tomoko’s report and the public meeting preceding the assembly vote. Several witnesses revise statements made earlier in the series.
10. “An Open File” resolves the immediate dispute over the investigation. The redevelopment is postponed for a survey, while the characters confront the consequences for their employment and family relationships.

= = Development = =

The project originated in a proposal submitted by producer Ayumi Kase to Tōkai Central Television in 2012. The proposal concerned a municipal employee who investigated changes in a town through ordinary administrative records. An early version placed the protagonist in a taxation office, but the setting was changed to an archive to allow documents from different departments and periods to enter the narrative.

Minase developed the scripts over approximately eighteen months. During research, she interviewed retired municipal employees, harbour workers, fish wholesalers, and local history volunteers. The production also consulted an archival adviser on storage practices, access restrictions, and the distinction between administrative records and privately deposited collections.

The fictional city of Mizunoe combined features of several medium-sized ports. Its harbour was designed as a place where commercial fishing, coastal freight, and passenger transport had expanded at different times. This allowed the redevelopment dispute to involve competing uses of the waterfront rather than a simple division between residents and an outside developer.

The principal story was outlined before filming began. However, the relationship between Fumie and Chie was expanded during script revisions. Their shared experience of relocation became central to explaining why some former residents opposed reopening the compensation proceedings.

The producers considered including a criminal prosecution in the final episode. This was abandoned because the available evidence within the story did not establish a single offence or responsible individual. The revised conclusion focused on the city’s obligation to investigate an uncertain property boundary and preserve the records supporting its decisions.

= = Production = =

Principal photography took place between June and September 2014. Exterior scenes were filmed in working harbours and shopping streets in Shizuoka Prefecture, while the municipal records office was constructed inside a disused administrative building. The production used separate locations for the fish market, municipal assembly chamber, and Saeki family home.

The archive set contained approximately 4,000 labelled boxes and bound volumes. Most were empty props, but documents handled in close-up were prepared in full. The art department created consistent address changes, signatures, and numbering systems so that papers shown in different episodes could be compared. Older documents were produced on several paper stocks to distinguish departmental forms from private correspondence.

Filming in the market was arranged around trading hours. Naruse spent several mornings observing fish vendors before production, and background performers included local traders. Scenes involving the harbour maintenance crew were supervised by technical advisers, particularly when actors worked near mooring equipment or inspected drainage outlets.

Sugita used a restrained visual style, with extended shots of conversations and relatively few handheld sequences. Office scenes frequently place several characters within the same frame, allowing routine work to continue behind the principal dialogue. Close-ups of documents usually appear after a character has identified their relevance.

The storm sequence in the seventh episode combined controlled water effects with footage recorded during poor weather earlier in production. The archive recovery scenes depict sorting, photographic documentation, and temporary drying arrangements. The production avoided showing soaked records being immediately unfolded or placed against direct heat.

= = Music and sound = =

The score was composed by Naoki Terase and performed by a small ensemble featuring piano, viola, clarinet, double bass, and percussion. Much of the music uses short recurring phrases rather than complete themes. A descending piano figure accompanies Tomoko’s solitary work, while a separate clarinet passage is associated with the market.

The opening title sequence shows the harbour at dawn, followed by close views of stamped forms, rope fibres, map folds, and handwritten corrections. Its instrumental theme, “Low Water”, changes slightly across the series through the addition of individual instruments.

The closing song, “Ashita no Kishibe” (“Tomorrow’s Shore”), was performed by singer-songwriter Emi Kurose. The full recording appears only in the final episode; earlier episodes use an abbreviated arrangement.

Environmental sound occupies a prominent place in the programme. Refrigeration motors, public address announcements, passing freight vehicles, and the municipal building’s ventilation system remain audible during dialogue. Several important archival discoveries occur without accompanying music.

A soundtrack album containing 23 tracks was released in December 2014. It included the opening theme and incidental music but omitted the vocal recording of the closing song.

= = Themes and analysis = =

The Tide Ledger examines the relationship between official documentation and personal recollection. Records provide evidence unavailable through memory alone, but their meaning depends on the circumstances in which they were created. The compensation ledger is accurate about the amounts paid while remaining incomplete about the people who ultimately received them.

The series also distinguishes between the absence of a record and evidence that an event did not occur. Tomoko repeatedly encounters activities conducted through informal arrangements, including shared accommodation and the collection of payments by neighbours. Her investigation becomes more reliable as she learns to describe these gaps without filling them through assumption.

Generational conflict is developed through the Saeki family. Tomoko regards disclosure as a necessary condition of a fair decision. Fumie associates the same inquiry with the risk of renewed displacement and public judgement of choices made under financial pressure. Neither position remains unchanged: Tomoko moderates some claims in her report, while Fumie accepts that the earlier settlement cannot determine current land rights without examination.

Employment is another recurring subject. Tomoko’s temporary contract limits her authority, Kenta depends on subcontracted delivery work, and Tsuji studies for an examination while performing duties similar to those of permanent staff. These circumstances shape how openly each character can challenge the redevelopment.

The title refers both to the compensation book and to the harbour’s repeated alteration by tides, engineering, and administrative decisions. Shots of the same quay at different water levels establish that visible conditions can change without the underlying structure becoming immediately apparent.

= = Broadcast and reception = =

The series aired on Thursdays at 10 p.m. on Tōkai Central Television and affiliated regional stations. Episodes became available through the network’s catch-up service the following day. A daytime repeat was broadcast in summer 2015, accompanied by short segments on the creation of the programme’s fictional documents.

Audience measurement within the network’s principal broadcast region recorded an average household rating of 7.4 per cent. The first episode received 6.8 per cent, while the finale reached 9.1 per cent. Catch-up viewing increased during the second half of the run, particularly after the seventh episode.

Reviews generally praised the performances of Serizawa and Naruse, whose scenes were noted for presenting disagreement without lengthy explanatory speeches. The ensemble cast and attention to ordinary municipal work also received favourable comment. Several reviewers identified the fifth and eighth episodes as important shifts in the story’s treatment of the original settlement.

Criticism focused on the pace of the opening episodes and the difficulty of following the relationships between different maps and registers. Some reviewers considered the unresolved redevelopment outcome appropriate to the subject, while others found that it limited the dramatic effect of the finale.

The programme received a regional television craft award for production design in 2015. Minase was also shortlisted for a television writing prize for the final two episodes.

= = Home media and special = =

A five-disc DVD edition was released in April 2015. Supplementary material included cast interviews, a location feature, and a short documentary explaining how the prop documents were designed. An accompanying booklet reproduced selected maps and ledger pages with annotations describing their place in the story.

Minase adapted the scripts into a novel published later that year. The adaptation retains the television ending but adds sections from Fumie’s perspective and a fuller account of Tomoko’s former museum employment.

The 92-minute special The Tide Ledger: Spring Soundings aired on 20 March 2016. Set fifteen months after the series, it follows the deposit of a privately maintained harbour radio log in the municipal archive. The log raises questions about the ownership of a former rescue station, while preparations for the market’s relocation place renewed pressure on the Saeki family.

The special confirms that the independent survey led to changes in the terminal design and the retention of a public waterfront route. It does not present the redevelopment as complete. Its closing scene shows volunteers identifying people in an uncatalogued photograph, continuing the original series’ emphasis on records as an ongoing public responsibility.
